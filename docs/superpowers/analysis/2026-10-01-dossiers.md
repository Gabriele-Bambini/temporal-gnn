## 1. What it actually is

**Object being learned.** A deterministic, autonomous velocity field v_θ(x, c).
- It is an MLP with 3 layers of 128 units and SiLU activations (`models/vector_field.py::MLPVectorField`).
- x is a cell's standardised log-normalised expression: about 1,200–1,430 genes on real data (1000 HVG ∪ CollecTRI TFs, min_detect 0.01, chosen on fit cells only), 40 genes on the simulator.
- c is an 8-dim learned condition embedding. There is no time input: `clock="duration"` and `field_time="none"`, and `_context_mode` returns "none", so the context is the condition only.
- Standardiser and gene set are fitted on fit cells only (`fit_on_train`).

**Prediction.** Observed source cells at t_a are integrated with RK4 over the horizon h = t_b − t_a, at 20 steps per unit time (`FlowModel.predict_standardized`). The output is one predicted cell per source cell. Biological time enters only as the integration length and as the spline parameter during training ("two clocks").

**Path construction** (`trainer.spline_paths`, `coupling.chain_ot`, `path.SplinePath`):
1. Per (condition, replicate) group, subsample n = min(|X_t|, 1000) cells at every fit time.
2. Chain exact Hungarian OT assignments t0→t1→…→tm (squared Euclidean cost in standardised space) into n pseudo-trajectories. This is balanced: no growth and no unbalanced mass.
3. Fit a natural cubic spline through each pseudo-trajectory in biological time.
4. v0.1.10 adds `center="linear"`: subtract the spline-of-means and add back piecewise-linear interpolation of the knot means, keeping each trajectory's deviation.

**Training, stage 1** (flow matching on the path):
- Regress v(x + σε) on the spline's velocity at u ~ U[t0, tm], with σ_min = 0.01.
- Adam with lr 1e-3, batch 256, up to 8000 iterations, EMA 0.999, gradient clipping at 1.
- Early stopping on the validation-timepoint ED every 250 iterations, patience 8.

**Training, stage 2** (`trainer._finetune_marginal`, from v0.1.9):
- 1000 iterations at lr 3e-4.
- Each step pushes a batch of 256 cells of X_{t_a} through the field (8 RK4 steps per unit) and minimises a differentiable U-statistic energy distance to an independent batch of X_{t_b}, over observed consecutive transitions (`flows/marginal.py`).
- The path loss is kept at weight 1 but restricted to ±1 time unit around the knots. This is TrajectoryNet/MIOFlow-style marginal matching added on top of CFM.

**Nested design.**
- The test time is removed entirely.
- An inner validation time sets the stage-1 iteration count.
- The model is then refit on all observed times for that iteration count, plus 1000 stage-2 iterations with no validation guard (`marginal_guarded=False`). That unguarded refit makes every reported prediction from v0.1.9 onwards.
- Configurations are selected on 2 inner validation folds.

**Conditioning in practice.**
- Real data have a single WT condition.
- Replicates define coupling groups. They are pooled when labels don't link stages (Argelaguet, Pijuan-Sala). In Veres, the differentiation is the replicate.
- Perturbation conditions are exercised only in toys and simulator knockouts. They are never tested on real data; the Argelaguet Brachyury KO was set aside.

**Unused or inert parts.**
- `data/prior.py::GRNPrior` (with degree-preserving randomisation) and `real.load_collectri`: the prior only forces TFs into the gene set. `eb_benchmark.py` lines 89–124 print coverage and nothing consumes it.
- No gated GRN field exists anywhere in the code: there is no `grn_field.py`, `gate_mode` or gate metric.
- `grn_truth.npz` (73 edges, 42 switching) is written but never scored.

**Code state:** v0.1.11. 192 tests pass as of 09-27. Twelve versions (0.1.0–0.1.11) in about a week.

## 3. Diagnosed failure modes and their structural reasons

**F1. The path is the ceiling (simulator).**
- The path family scored with no network is 0.636; the trained model is 0.641, so architecture plus optimiser contribute 0.005.
- Adding the missing knot moves the ceiling to 0.422, and the model reaches 0.428.
- The error is pure location: shifting any prediction onto the true mean takes ED to the noise floor (0.636 → −0.003; persistence 4.155 → +0.003). For mean errors below 3, ED ≈ 0.34–0.36 × (mean error)².

**F2. Spline of means (an algebraic identity).** Chaining is a bijection over equal-size subsamples, and a cubic spline is linear in its knots. So the path's mean at any time equals the spline through the snapshot means, for any coupling rule. Measured: OT and random chaining give mean errors 1.3337591265 and 1.3337591278.
- OT can therefore only change shape, never the centroid.
- Any interpolant that is linear in the snapshot data has an affine-combination-of-means centroid. Even the best affine combination chosen knowing the true answer leaves a mean error of 0.802 (ED ≥ 0.23).

**F3. The linear centre makes the training target's centroid equal to the strongest control's.** With `center="linear"`, the path mean at the held-out time is exactly the linear interpolation between the bracketing knot means. That is mean_interp's centroid (gastrulation: path mean error 8.46 vs mean_interp 8.44).
- ED in this space is centroid-dominated. In Job E, base's ED splits into a mean part of 0.153 and a trace part of 0.009 on Veres, and 0.298 vs 0.003 on Pijuan-Sala.
- So stage 1 can at best tie mean interpolation unless shape matters. Any "win" must come from the network or stage 2 moving the mean, which can just as easily hurt: on Veres, base's mean error is 2.74 against mean_interp's 2.25.
- This is the core reason the fresh results are a tie and a negative.

**F4. Uncalibrated spread.**
- Path var_ratio at the target is 1.54 (Argelaguet E8.0), 1.23 (Veres d4), 1.11 (Pijuan-Sala E8.0) and 1.71 (Pijuan-Sala secondary).
- The OT interpolant contracts (0.56–0.68): each of its cells averages two cells. Mean_interp keeps the spread (0.95–1.00).
- No deterministic construction gets the spread right. The no-network fixes (variance rescaling, Brownian-bridge σ) flip sign between validation folds and test in both directions, so none was adopted.

**F5. The bottleneck moves between path and network.**
- Simulator: network equals path within ±0.005.
- EB test: path 0.130 vs model 0.267. On 3 of 4 folds the network loses 0.18–0.22 of its target.
- Argelaguet: the path itself loses (1.938 vs 1.513).
- Veres: the path loses badly (0.190 vs 0.068); the model (0.115) beats its own path only because of stage 2.

**F6. Autonomous deterministic field.** v(x) has to average the velocities of trajectories that pass the same state at different times.
- This works on the simulator because state determines time there: there is a common start, noise 0.05, and no revisits.
- On Veres, stage 1 alone (noft 0.182) equals persistence (0.188) and the shuffled twin (0.182), with mean error 3.284 vs 3.300 for persistence: no net displacement over day 3→4. My hypothesis is that overlapping progenitor states make the averaged velocity cancel. It was not tested: `field_time=bio` was run only with stage 2 on, and time_noft was inconclusive.
- A flow map is also injective, so one state cannot split into two fates. Branching can only come from heterogeneity already present in the source.

**F7. The stage that makes all reported predictions is never validated.** The refit's 1000 stage-2 iterations run without a guard; the docstring originally claimed a guard, which was corrected in Step 0.
- The nested guard rejected stage 2 in 6/6 seeds on short folds, yet the refit ran it anyway.
- Its effect depends on fold geometry: it helps on long horizons (noft worse in 18/18 long pairs) and is mixed on short ones.
- The transfer arm (stopping at the nested iteration) ≈ base (P +0.005).

**F8. Selection with 5 timepoints.** That leaves 2 inner folds. Horizon, position in the gap, boundary vs interior source, path dispersion, nested-fit knot count and source compactness are all collinear.
- A single validation fold mis-selected the path family (v0.1.6).
- Validation-vs-test disagreements ran in both directions.
- Fresh-fold multi-removal designs (Job D) partly decorrelate these, and the result was "no arm qualifies".

**F9. Representation and metric dependence.**
- EB: −0.104 in gene space, +0.002 in PCA-50.
- In 5-D whitened PCs, mean_interp falls to 0.861 W1 and the OT interpolant becomes the strongest control. Which control is "best" flips with the representation.
- Rare-gene spikes (41% / 33% of genes detected in under 1% of cells; |z| up to 263,822) dominated the OT cost but moved ED only from 1.3234 to 1.3235. ED is insensitive to single-cell outliers that wreck the couplings.

**F10. Extrapolation fails.** Absolute time in the context diverged (v0.1.1), and the terminal forecast was unstable. Nothing beyond the last observed time is supported.

**F11. The simulator cannot discriminate shape.** Per-gene spread is 0.21–0.26 while the population travels about 1 unit per unit time, so snapshots are near-translates. Every synthetic win is a location win. The simulator's ground truth is also non-autonomous (switching edges) while the model is autonomous; it passes only because of F6.

## 4. Against the user goal (scores 0–5)

**Generative: 2/5.**
- For: it outputs full cell-level populations, scored with distributional metrics (ED, MMD, W2, var_ratio, mean/trace split). That is better than point prediction.
- Against: it is a deterministic pushforward of real source cells.
  - It cannot sample de novo and produces exactly as many cells as there are source cells.
  - It has no noise model, density or likelihood, and no growth or death (balanced OT, equal subsamples).
  - It cannot represent stochastic fate choice.
- On fresh data its populations are no better than "shift the source cells by the mean change" (Veres worse, Pijuan-Sala tied).
- It is far from the replicate floor: Veres 0.115 vs floor 0.051 (gap_closed −2.68); Argelaguet 2.03 vs pool distance 0.027.

**Quasi-continuous: 2/5.**
- For: the field can be integrated to any horizon, and the training paths are continuous in biological time.
- Against:
  - It is validated only at held-out snapshot times. There, piecewise-linear mean interpolation is as good or better.
  - The path centroid is piecewise-linear by construction, so "between snapshots" means linear interpolation of means plus a deterministic shape.
  - The field is time-homogeneous.
  - Confirmatory horizons are 0.125–0.14 of the span.
  - Extrapolation is unstable.
  - There is no evaluation at truly intermediate times.

**Interpretable: 0/5.**
- No parameter has biological meaning: a 3×128 MLP on about 1,300 standardised genes plus an 8-dim embedding.
- The CollecTRI prior only selects genes.
- The Phase-3 gated signed GRN (spec dated 2026-09-18) was never implemented, and the simulator's ground-truth edge schedules were never scored.
- There is no Jacobian or attribution analysis, no real perturbation prediction, and no extracted states, rates or fates.

Is anything interpretable at all? Only the diagnostics of failure: the path-vs-model ceiling, the mean/trace split and var_ratio. There is also the weak claim that "the model uses time": it beats the shuffled twin by −0.067 on Veres and −0.240 on Pijuan-Sala, but stage 1 on Veres does not.

## 5. What to KEEP and what NOT to repeat

**KEEP: protocol**
- Two separate clocks.
- Splits by whole biological unit, with couplings computed within groups.
- Mandatory naive controls. Mean interpolation is the decisive one; also persistence, the McCann/OT interpolant, the shuffled-time twin, replicate floors and the "fraction of gap closed".
- U-statistic ED with a permutation null; the mean/trace split; var_ratio; error of the mean.
- No-network ceiling diagnostics: score the training target itself before training anything. This was the single most informative tool.
- Pre-registration with SHA-256 freeze, rules applied by code, deviations logged with the original rule's outcome, and confirmation on fresh datasets (Veres, Pijuan-Sala).
- Bit-exact reproducibility gate and dual checkpoints (stage-1 vs final arms at zero extra cost).
- Multi-removal (skip-k) fold designs (`training/designs.py`).
- Gene detection filter (min_detect 0.01) and a standardiser fitted on fit cells only.
- Data cards.

**KEEP: reusable code**
- `flows/coupling.py` (`ot_coupling`, `chain_ot`)
- `flows/marginal.py` (differentiable U-statistic ED, sliced W2)
- `flows/solver.py`
- `evaluation/metrics.py` and `controls.py`
- `data/real.py` (sparse CSR loaders, `select_genes`, `load_collectri`)
- `data/prior.py` (degree-preserving randomised prior, useful later)
- `data/synthetic_grn.py` (known edge schedules, but it needs heterogeneity)
- Parsed HPC caches: Veres (`~/rafm_data/processed/veres2019_stage5.npz`), Pijuan-Sala (`pijuan_sala2019_atlas.npz`), Argelaguet, EB.

**DO NOT repeat**
1. Building ever more elaborate interpolation paths that are linear in snapshot data. The centroid is algebraically pinned (F2, F3), and the linear centre literally reproduces the strongest control's centroid.
2. Proposing candidate fixes after looking at the test window (EB v0.1.10), and churning versions on 2 inner folds.
3. A black-box autonomous deterministic MLP over about 1,300 standardised genes as the core object. It is uninterpretable, cannot represent nonstationarity or branching, and stage 1 can stall (Veres).
4. Deferring the scientific object (mechanism and interpretability) while optimising a centroid-dominated metric on which mean interpolation is near-optimal.
5. Validating on a simulator whose snapshots are near-translates.
6. Running an unguarded training stage that produces the reported predictions.
7. Comparing against published numbers without a calibration gate (it failed here).
8. Reading tight seed-only CIs as evidence of biological generalisation.
9. Spike genes in standardised space feeding OT costs.

## 6. Hidden risks the user may not see

1. **ED is mostly "predict the mean".** In about 1,300 standardised dimensions, the ED mean part is 10–100× the trace part (Job E). Mean interpolation is therefore near-optimal by construction, and shape, multimodality and fate proportions barely register. `ed_approx` also deviates from measured ED (Veres 0.162 vs 0.115), so the split is only approximate. A generative model that is better on shape can still lose.
2. **Matching marginals does not identify dynamics.** All metrics compare marginals. Two fields with identical marginals but different trajectories, couplings or regulators are indistinguishable. Any claim of interpretable dynamics needs evidence beyond the marginals: lineage barcodes, metabolic labelling, RNA velocity, perturbations or ATAC.
3. **No lineage information anywhere.** OT pseudo-trajectories are never checked against ground-truth clones (e.g. Weinreb LARRY). Balanced OT with equal subsampling ignores proliferation and death and drops cells (n ≤ 1000 per snapshot).
4. **Too few timepoints.** EB and Argelaguet have 5 (2 inner folds). Veres has 8 and Pijuan-Sala 9, but the confirmatory horizon is still one grid step. Continuous-time claims rest on interpolating across one gap.
5. **Replicate floors are broken or missing.** EB has no biological replicate. The two Pijuan-Sala E8.0 pools sit in different sequencing batches (floor 1.105, above every model). In Argelaguet the floor is 0.027 against 1.5 for the best method. "How good is good enough" is undefined on most datasets.
6. **Representation dependence.** Gene-space vs PCA vs 5-D whitened rankings flip, and results depend on HVG count, TF union, filter and scaler. Any headline number is conditional on these choices.
7. **Leakage-adjacent history.** The EB positive's configuration came from a no-network diagnosis on the EB test window, and the Job B windows were selection folds. Only Veres and Pijuan-Sala are clean, and they are a negative and a tie.
8. **Extrapolation is untested or negative.** Any "beyond measured timepoints" requirement in the user goal has no supporting evidence.
9. **The "regulatory" part has delivered nothing.**
   - CollecTRI only selects genes, and Phase 3 is unimplemented.
   - The spec itself concedes that time-varying edge gates may be unidentifiable from marginals and would need anchors such as ATAC (the chromatin half of the Argelaguet multiome is unused).
   - The prior's support is also limited to selected genes.
   - The sibling Dileo GNN audit closed negative: AUPRC 0.94 was a sampler artefact, and GNNs scored 0.50 under degree-matched negatives.
10. **The shuffled-time twin is a weak control.** Beating it shows only that some temporal ordering is used. It says nothing about biological mechanism.
11. **Process cost and statistical power.** Job E took 3–5 h per dataset under a QoS of 1 running and 2 submitted jobs. The pre-registration machinery (R-SEL, R-HOR, Rule B, gates) is heavy relative to the effect sizes, which are about 0.01–0.05 ED on fresh data. With 5 seeds and 2 datasets, power to detect real but small gains is low.
12. **Integrator, capacity and time-input fixes are already exhausted.** 8 vs 20 steps are identical, width 512 is worse, and time input is null. Further tuning of this architecture has little left to give. The limits are structural: F2, F3, F4 and F6.

### 1. One paragraph per project

**tgl-grn-1CCD: replication of Dileo & Sottoriva's temporal GNN for GRNs.**
- **Question:** can temporal GNNs predict how a GRN changes over time? Three tasks: link prediction at t+1, log-delta gene expression at t+1, and future out-degree centrality.
- **Data:** aggregate GRNs, not cells. Each pseudotime (DPT) bin gets its own unpruned GRNBoost2/SCENIC importance matrix, plus per-gene bin statistics.
  - Pancreas: 12 snapshots, 7,197 nodes, 662–663 regulators, 0.9–1.0M edges per snapshot (about 20% of all TF×gene pairs).
  - Gastrulation: 32 snapshots, 6,345 nodes, 525–582 regulators.
  - Cells per bin range from 5 to 922 (184×) on gastrulation and 50 to 698 on pancreas.
  - Only 23–27% of edges recur between adjacent bins (`logs-baseline\NEGATIVE_SAMPLING_AUDIT_2026-09-17.md` §1, `AUTOPSY.md` §9.3).
- **Model:** a zoo of 12+ models (GCRN-GRU, EvolveGCN, ROLAND, ChebNet/GCN/GAT, scGPT/scFoundation, EdgeBank).
- **Outcome:** the paper's AUPRC of 0.94 replicated: 0.936–0.938 ± 0.001 over 3 seeds (`results\results.md`, `AUTOPSY.md` §10.4). It then collapsed under the audit:
  - A learning-free "source out-degree" scorer gets 0.940 (pancreas) and 0.944 (gastrulation), because 92.4% of the paper's negative edges start from a gene that never regulates anything.
  - With degree-matched negatives, GCRN-GRU, ChebNet and EdgeBank all score 0.500–0.507, std ≤ 0.004, and no snapshot reaches 0.60.
  - On new edges they score 0.435–0.49, which is below chance (HPC job 661895, `logs-baseline\hpc_ns_audit_summary.md`).
  - Centrality: persistence reaches Spearman 0.996/0.998, against 0.499 for the best model.
  - Expression: the "predict no change" baseline has the lowest MAE everywhere, and the deltas on the human datasets are anti-correlated (−0.46 and −0.39).
  - The "temporal" models were static. GConvGRU was always called with H=0, leaving 49% of its parameters unused, and EvolveGCN's state evolved across training epochs rather than across snapshots (§10.3).
- **Root cause:** the data cannot support the claim. Each bin's GRN is a noisy importance matrix inferred separately, so there is no evolving object to predict. The metric measured the negative sampler, not the models.

  - Degree-product heuristic: 0.938, equal to the GNN. On a future graph that was randomly rewired with degrees preserved it still scores 0.93–0.95 (T7). The AUPRC is therefore geometry of the degree sequence.

  - T9: adding graph-neighbour aggregates to a ridge model of next-bin expression gives −0.003 R². Even oracle neighbours from the future graph give +0.0002.
  - T10: hub turnover is the only real signal. Jaccard(top-100) between consecutive bins is 0.005–0.036, against a bootstrap noise floor of 0.157–0.241.
  - T11: that turnover is only weakly learnable. HGB beats persistence by +0.025 ρ (p = 0.043) and recovers about 7% of the true future top-100.
- **Root cause:** one sentence in `SIGNAL_VERDICT.md` sums it up: with bin-level statistics, no architecture can extract regulatory dynamics through this graph. `TEMPORAL_SIGNAL_PLAN.md` draws the right conclusion: change what time has to predict before changing the architecture, with explicit kill criteria S1–S4.

**cfm_grn: the first flow-matching prototype.**
- **Question:** a perturbation question (control cells → knockout cells), not a temporal one. It asks whether conditional flow matching with a graph prior and a learned contextual activity mask `M_θ` predicts unseen knockouts.
- **Data:** cell-level but synthetic. 30 genes, 300 cells per condition, Hill kinetics plus a Langevin SDE, 15 knockouts in 5 folds.
- **Outcome by version:**
  - **v1** (`output\benchmark_held_out_results.csv`): Graph_CFM energy distance 15.06 vs persistence 12.23. Pearson 0.849 vs 0.937, W1 1.118 vs 0.727. The plain static graph (10.05) also beat the proposed model.
  - **v2** (`output\results_v2\_aggregate_output.txt`, job 667180, 315 runs):
    - The root causes were that global OT mixed conditions (ED_de 14.2 vs 6.5 once OT was done per condition) and that absolute-level metrics favour persistence.
    - After the fix, the models learn only to knock down the target gene. Downstream Pearson is about 0 for every model.
    - The contextual mask is not better than one learned weight per edge (comparison P5, not significant or worse).
  - **v3/v4** (`results_v3\_summary_v3.csv`):
    - A signed prior lifts downstream Pearson from −0.07 to 0.41–0.54.
    - A mechanistic model that learns dx/dt from velocities and is integrated after clamping the knocked-out gene reaches 0.45–0.68; the true simulator drift reaches 0.999.
    - The same mechanistic model without a graph prior fails (0.08, MSE 7.8).
    - Velocity noise at SNR 1 drops the unsigned mechanistic model to 0.11.
    - The learned mask barely tells true edges from spurious ones (edge AUC 0.538); velocity-based fitting gets 0.71–0.76.
- **Root cause:** the deciding factors were the data regime (sign, velocities) and the coupling bug, not the architecture. It also drifted away from the temporal goal.

**tgl-grn-continuous: newest prototype, 2026-09-30, written by Codex.**
- **Question:** is there temporal signal left in the aggregate files, beyond persistence and temporal nulls?
- **Data:** the same aggregate files. Per gene and per bin: mean, median, std and fraction non-zero; `sum` is excluded. The graph is reduced to the top 8 targets per regulator by streaming the 11.3M (pancreas) and 25.9M (gastrulation) edge CSVs.
- **Model:** a weighted mean encoder separated by edge direction, a per-gene GRU over 3 snapshots with BPTT, and a velocity MLP. Trained in two ways: a direct Euler step, and gene-to-gene identity-paired "flow matching" integrated with the midpoint method.
- **Controls:** persistence, momentum, ridge, expression-only, static, shuffled-history and rewired variants. Chronological whole-snapshot splits, a scaler fitted on the training prefix only, and causal confidence smoothing weighted by √n_cells.
- **Outcome:** none yet. ATTEMPTS A-004 is RUNNING, there is no results directory, and only unit tests pass (16 + 13 + 16 tests).
- **Root cause, predicted:** it re-asks a question already answered by T6/T9/T11 on the same files. T9 shows a graph lift of zero even with the oracle future graph.

### 2. Cross-cutting anti-patterns

1. **Aggregate summaries instead of cells.** Every real-data attempt used per-bin GRN matrices and per-gene marginal statistics. That removes heterogeneity, the gene–gene joint distribution, lineages and cell counts; bins of 5 cells sit in the training set. This is unrecoverable by design (`.projectos\STATE.md` blockers; NS audit §6.1.4).
2. **The graph is inherited, not modelled.** Each bin gets an independent, unpruned GRNBoost2 matrix with about 77% edge turnover. 92% of the sampled negative edges were impossible by construction, and 87% of "future" edges were already in the history (`SIGNAL_VERDICT.md` T2/T3/T8).
3. **Sampler and protocol artefacts read as signal.** AUPRC 0.94 equals the source-out-degree heuristic, and degree-product survives a randomly rewired future (NS audit §3; T7).
4. **Trivial baselines missing or added too late.** Persistence 0.996 vs model 0.499 (`AUTOPSY.md` §9.1). The zero-change baseline wins on MAE (§9.2). Persistence beat Graph_CFM in cfm_grn v1.
5. **"Temporal" models that are not temporal.** H=0 on every call; EvolveGCN's state evolves across epochs; `detach` blocks backprop through time; A3TGCN is fed repeated x. Then the threading fix without backprop through time was judged and found flat (§10.3, §10.5).
6. **Real time thrown away.** Pseudotime bins were used although Pijuan-Sala has embryo stages (E-MTAB-6967). `bin_mid` and `n_cells_bin` were dropped by the loader. Bins are nearly evenly spaced (max/min gap 1.19–1.25×), so Δt-aware designs buy nothing on these files.
7. **Tautological or misaligned targets.** "Future" centrality is computed on the same snapshot. The last target column is all zeros. A classification split (`RandomNodeSplit`) was used on a continuous target. Nodes were split inside the same future snapshot (AUTOPSY M-5/M-6; T4).
8. **Single-seed claims.** Run-to-run spread is about 0.004, against a 3-seed CI of ±0.0009. ChebNet on gastrulation has std 0.10, with seeds at 0.70, 0.86 and 0.93.
9. **Positive results announced before the controls.** "+0.144 over EdgeBank = real signal" (AUTOPSY §10.2) was overturned to 0.50. `results\results.md` also presents hub heatmaps from a model that persistence beats by about 2×.
10. **Architecture-first iteration on a target with no signal.** Directed layers, recurrence, ROLAND, contextual masks and flow matching did not change the verdict. The changes that did were in data and target: sign, velocities, hub turnover.
11. **Question drift with no stated goal.** The question moved from link prediction to centrality, then expression, then perturbation (cfm_grn, which is not temporal), then aggregate gene-state forecasting. `.projectos\PURPOSE.md` is still an empty template. None of these attempts ever modelled a population of cells over time.
12. **"Flow matching" without its distributional part.** In cfm_grn v1, global OT mixed labels. In tgl-grn-continuous, a deterministic identity pairing means there is no source distribution, so the model does regression, not generation.
13. **Repository sprawl.** Five near-identical forks with copied AUTOPSY files and no canonical snapshot (`MAP.md`, open questions). HPC code was ahead of local code (NS audit §5). `results.md` documents a `detach` hack that `AUTOPSY.md` §8 says was removed.
14. **The laptop dictated the science.** The top-8-per-regulator reduction exists because of 1.81–6.51 GB of local RAM on CPU (plan, self-review). The Bocconi HPC was available.
15. **Interpretability claimed but never validated.** The learned mask has edge AUC 0.53–0.60. Hub heatmaps were drawn from non-predictive models.

### 3. Good patterns worth keeping

- **Adversarial controls that can kill a hypothesis:**
  - degree-matched and same-source negatives;
  - degree-preserving rewiring of the future graph;
  - oracle-future and shuffled-neighbour features (T9);
  - a bootstrap noise floor (T10).
- **Learning-free scorers first, with exact replication of published numbers.** The local EdgeBank reproduced the paper's 0.7911 as 0.7912.
- **Pre-registered paired comparisons.** cfm_grn v2 P1–P6 use folds × seeds, 95% CI, Wilcoxon tests and win counts. Multi-seed runs write the seed into the file name.
- **Written kill criteria and decision gates** (`TEMPORAL_SIGNAL_PLAN.md` §2, §8).
- **Synthetic ground truth with a ceiling model.** Oracle / noisy / noisy_context scenarios, with `mech_true` at 0.999 as the ceiling.
- **Effect-based metrics:** delta Pearson on downstream genes, direction accuracy, knockdown fraction, energy distance on differentially expressed genes. Not absolute levels.
- **Leakage-safe engineering in tgl-grn-continuous:**
  - chronological whole-snapshot splits and a training-prefix scaler;
  - a test that changing the future cannot alter past context;
  - n_cells used as a confidence weight, not as a feature;
  - skill reported relative to persistence;
  - momentum that handles unequal gaps;
  - streaming edge cache with a bounded memory footprint;
  - conservative limitations written into `report.md`.
- **Opt-in flags** that never silently change the behaviour of existing HPC scripts.
- **Hub turnover identified as the one real-data task where persistence fails** (p@10 of persistence = 0.0 on PageRank).
- **The levers that worked:** a signed prior, velocity-like observations, and keeping a graph prior for extrapolation to unseen perturbations (`results_v3\_summary_v3.csv`).

### 4. Can tgl-grn-continuous reach the goal by construction? No.

- **Data granularity.**
  - The model sees about 7k (pancreas) or about 6k (gastrulation) genes × 4 marginal statistics × 12 or 32 bins.
  - That is one deterministic multivariate time series, not a population. There are no cells and no gene–gene covariance. Pancreas lineages are pooled inside each DPT bin.
  - So it cannot sample cell states or populations, represent branching or fates, or estimate proportions. The spec admits this; the generated report says it is "not cell trajectories".
- **What "generative" means there: nothing.**
  - `fm_loss` interpolates in a straight line, with each gene's identity-paired source and target: z = x_s + τ(x_next − x_s). There is one endpoint per gene and no noise or source distribution.
  - The regression target is therefore the displacement itself, and the integrated ODE is a deterministic point forecast. It is functionally a reparametrised direct model, and the `models.py` docstring states it performs no optimal transport.
  - There is no sampling API.
- **What "continuous" means there.**
  - `bin_mid` enters as a scalar, and `forecast` integrates over any `delta_s`.
  - But training uses only one-step gaps that are nearly uniform. Paths between snapshots are straight lines by construction, so a query between bins can only return a linear interpolation of the same snapshots.
  - Evaluation is teacher-forced rolling one-step prediction, not a free rollout, so extrapolation is never tested.
  - The clock is pseudotime over pooled cells, not real time.
  - The test set is tiny: the split in `temporal.py` gives pancreas 6 train, 2 validation and 3 test transitions; gastrulation gets about 6 test transitions. Quick mode uses 1 seed.
- **Interpretability.**
  - The graph is the inherited GRNBoost2 importance matrix, cut to the top 8 targets per regulator purely to save memory.
  - Aggregation uses unsigned weighted means. There are no per-edge learned parameters, signs, rates or regulator→transition attributions. The core is a GRU plus MLP.
  - T9 already showed on these files that graph neighbours add −0.003 R² (+0.0002 even with the oracle future graph). `graph_flow` vs `expression_only_flow` will most likely tie.
- **Verdict machinery.**
  - `evaluation.evidence_verdict` always returns `"exploratory_only"`. The spec's three states (signal_recovered, weak_or_task_specific, not_recovered) were never implemented, so the prototype can never output a positive or negative verdict.
  - The spec's "target-time permutation" null was replaced by `shuffled_history_flow`, which shuffles only the order of past states.
- **Best case:** it re-confirms T6/T11. The aggregate state is predictable mostly through mean reversion (ridge R² 0.77–0.86), with a small hub-turnover lift. Reusable parts: the streaming cache, leakage tests, split, metrics and report template.

### 5. Top 10 lessons for the next design (ranked)

1. **Model cells with real capture times, not aggregate per-bin GRNs.** A population claim needs single-cell joint distributions at measured timepoints; aggregate files can't support one, even in principle. Evidence: `tgl-grn-1CCD\logs-baseline\NEGATIVE_SAMPLING_AUDIT_2026-09-17.md` §6.1.3–4; `tgl-grn-recurrent\SIGNAL_VERDICT.md` T9; `.projectos\STATE.md` blockers.
2. **Measure information content with cheap and oracle models before building any architecture.** If oracle-future features add nothing, no architecture will. Evidence: `SIGNAL_VERDICT.md` T9/T11; `tgl-grn-recurrent\TEMPORAL_SIGNAL_PLAN.md` §1.
3. **Every table carries trivial floors, and a model is reported only as its margin over the best one.** Floors: persistence, train mean, mean shift, momentum, ridge, replay, shuffled time, noise floor. Evidence: `tgl-grn-1CCD\AUTOPSY.md` §9.1–9.2; `cfm_grn\output\benchmark_held_out_results.csv`; `tgl-grn-exp\eval_baselines.py`.
4. **Attack every positive result with adversarial controls before believing it.** Use matched negatives, degree-preserving rewiring, an oracle future and shuffled time. Evidence: `tgl-grn-1CCD\logs-baseline\hpc_ns_audit_summary.md`; `SIGNAL_VERDICT.md` T7.
5. **Generative means distributional.** Use a stochastic source and minibatch OT coupling within each time pair, never mixing conditions or times. Score held-out timepoints with energy distance, W1 or MMD against replay and mean-shift controls. Evidence: `cfm_grn\output\results_v2\_aggregate_output.txt` P1 (global vs per-condition OT); `cfm_grn_v2\README.md` (control list).
6. **Test "continuous" where it can fail.** Hold out an intermediate timepoint for interpolation and the last timepoint for extrapolation, use free rollouts, and keep biological time separate from flow time. Evidence: `tgl-grn-continuous\src\tgl_grn_continuous\experiment.py` (report says the test is rolling one-step, not a free rollout); `cfm_grn_v2\README.md` (two clocks).
7. **Interpretable parameters must be scored against ground truth before any biological reading.** Check edge AUC, sign recovery and rates on a simulator with a known time-varying GRN. A learned mask at edge AUC 0.54 is noise. Evidence: `cfm_grn\output\results_v3\_summary_v3.csv`.
8. **Change the data regime before the architecture.** A signed prior and velocity-like observations moved downstream Pearson from −0.07 to 0.41–0.68. Directed layers, recurrence, masks and flow matching moved nothing. Evidence: `cfm_grn\output\results_v3\_summary_v3.csv`; `tgl-grn-1CCD\AUTOPSY.md` §10.5.
9. **Temporal modules must get gradient through time and must hurt when ablated.** Ablate with H=0 and with shuffled time order; otherwise they are decoration. Evidence: `tgl-grn-1CCD\AUTOPSY.md` §10.3, §10.5; `TEMPORAL_SIGNAL_PLAN.md` S3.
10. **Run statistics up front, keep one canonical repo and a filled-in PURPOSE.md.** Pre-register comparisons, use ≥3–5 seeds with CIs and paired tests, run heavy jobs on the HPC, and don't let laptop RAM decide the science. Evidence: `tgl-grn-1CCD\AUTOPSY.md` §10.4 (0.004 spread vs ±0.0009 CI); `.projectos\PURPOSE.md` (empty); `.projectos\MAP.md` (no canonical snapshot); `docs\superpowers\plans\2026-09-17-tgl-grn-continuous-prototype.md` (RAM-driven top-K).

## 2. Evaluation, one idea at a time

**I1 – Reframe to cell level** · enabling (none of G/C/I by itself)
- **For:** the diagnosis is correct. The distributed data are per-bin SCENIC aggregates with no cell×gene matrix, so no population generator can be trained on them. Real stage labels exist: Pijuan-Sala, 9 stages, E6.5–E8.5.
- **Against:** the bins use DPT pseudotime and the reconstruction uses real time, so the comparison mixes two time axes. The Sottoriva-lab asset is no longer central.
- **Assumption:** Dileo's "gastrulation" erythroid subset can be re-derived from E-MTAB-6967.
- **Identifiability:** not applicable.

**I2 – Persistence-aware audit of temporal-GNN link prediction** · evaluation only, not G/C/I
- **For:** cheap to do. [N] found no critique, v2 or citation of arXiv 2605.28659; Stock et al. 2025 covers only static GRNs, so the temporal case is unclaimed. The project memory index records GNNs at about 0.50 AUPRC under degree-matched negatives (not re-verified here).
- **Against:** it is a negative or benchmark paper about a v1 work-in-progress preprint, so its impact ceiling is low. It does not model cell populations at all.
- **Assumption:** the SCENIC bin sequences carry any real rewiring signal. Edge recurrence is 0.87 (gastrulation) and 0.68 (pancreas).
- **Identifiability:** edge "births and deaths" across bins partly reflect SCENIC thresholding noise. A null built from bootstrap SCENIC re-runs is needed before calling them rewiring.

**I3 – Static mask inside conditional FM, control→perturbed** · G only
- **For:** clean unseen-gene split; mature benchmarks.
- **Against:** no time. The mask has no observed endpoint. Ahlmann-Eltze and Systema show simple and linear baselines competitive. Representing an unseen gene is unsolved [R]. Superseded by RA-FM.
- **Identifiability:** a condition-level mask for an unseen perturbation P is undefined unless P has features.

**I4 – Multi-marginal FM generator** · G, C
- **For:** samples whole populations by pushing an observed marginal forward; simulation-free, so it scales.
- **Against:**
  - The regression target (velocity) is never observed. It comes from the OT coupling plus the chosen path (linear or spline), which encodes a least-action assumption.
  - Lim 2026 shows the FM optimum can simply replay observed transitions.
  - Real-data record [NX]: one positive (EB days 12–15) and one negative (Argelaguet E8.0).
  - The stage-2 effect depends on horizon, and 8 alternative explanations (H2–H9) are still open.
  - The fresh-fold tests remove *interior* stages (Veres day 4 from day 3; E8.0 from E7.75), so they test interpolation, not the terminal forecast that [P] requires.
- **Assumption:** OT pairing approximates true ancestor→descendant pairing at the given time gap.
- **Identifiability:** from marginals alone, the density-preserving (rotational) part of the velocity cannot be recovered (Weinreb et al. 2018 PNAS, [ext, UNVERIFIED]). Guan 2026 is positive only for gradient drift out of equilibrium [L], and a gated GRN drift is not a gradient.

**I5 – Growth** · I only if anchored; otherwise a nuisance for G
- **For:** proliferating or dying lineages need it, and WFR-FM makes it simulation-free.
- **Against:** scRNA-seq counts carry no absolute mass. The NX parsers cap cells at 1,500 per (day, differentiation) for Veres and 3,000 per stage for Pijuan-Sala, which removes total-mass information completely. [M] criterion 6 warns about exactly this.
- **Assumption:** composition shifts reflect differential proliferation rather than sampling or dissociation bias.
- **Identifiability:** only relative growth is recoverable, up to a time-dependent constant, and it trades off against transport through the WFR hyperparameter. It becomes identifiable with clone sizes (Watermelon barcodes) or with a proliferation-signature prior.

**I6 – SDE vs ODE** · G (calibrated spread)
- **For:** [P] proposes `Σ(X)` noise; NX shows spread matters: var_ratio is 1.22/1.13 on the folds stage 2 hurt vs 0.82/0.79 on the folds it helped.
- **Against:** [S3] is deterministic, so population spread comes only from the source sample. Over- or under-dispersion is a live failure mode.
- **Identifiability:** Guan gives diffusivity only for gradient drift out of equilibrium; a state-dependent `Σ(X)` with non-gradient drift is open.

**I7 – Signed prior-supported drift** · I; also C, if the prior is right
- **For:**
  - Parameters mean something (sign, weight, decay).
  - It shrinks the function class, which works against replay.
  - It makes interventions structural.
- **Against:**
  - A mask on the prior cannot recover edges outside it [R §7].
  - Non-TF drivers (signalling, ligands) are absent.
  - CollecTRI signs are often defaults.
  - The decay term acts on **standardised** values, so a gene below its mean gets positive "decay". λ is then no longer a degradation rate, and κ absorbs the offset.
- **Assumption:** TF mRNA tracks TF activity through `ψ_j(x_j)`. This breaks for post-translational control, and RAF1:ER is literally post-translational activation.
- **Identifiability:**
  - Only the product `w·m` is identified; the static part of m is confounded with w.
  - For each target, only the sum of incoming messages is constrained, so co-expressed regulators, which are typical of lineage programmes, cannot be separated.

**I8 – Gates m(t,c)** · I (C weakly)
- **For:** this is the scientific object. Low rank means r shared temporal programmes, which is interpretable at programme level. Starting from a static network is a sound initialisation.
- **Against:**
  - The spec drops x, so there is no lineage-specific rewiring at a given stage, even though gastrulation has many co-existing lineages; state-dependence enters only through c.
  - Restoring x turns the gate into a learned nonlinear interaction, which brings the model close to "Jacobian of a black box" (PFM/Navigo/DynaVelo) and erases the stated differentiation.
  - With r = 2–4, edge-level claims about "when" reduce to loadings on 2–4 modes.
- **Assumption:** rewiring is edge-specific and sparse in time.
- **Identifiability:**
  - Rotation invariance of (E, z) is harmless.
  - Time resolution is at most K−1 intervals: 3 for RENGE, 2 for CD4.
  - Where a switch falls inside an interval is set by TV plus the path, not by data. gate_switch_mae is itself defined on the observed grid.

**I9 – L1 + TV regime prior** · I
- **For:** a sensible Occam prior that matches "regime" biology.
- **Against:** with 4 observed times on a 16-point grid, switch placement is a regulariser artefact. The 16-config grid is selected on validation ED, which favours prediction, not recovery.

**I10 – Biological time only through the gates** · I, only with extra controls
- **For:** clean attribution. Every non-autonomy must be an edge switch, so the claim is falsifiable.
- **Against:** the gates become a sink for everything time-varying that is not modelled:
  - per-stage batch effects (NX: the two E8.0 pools are in different sequencing batches);
  - external stimuli (4-OHT in RAF1, TCR stimulation in CD4, morphogens);
  - cell-cycle composition;
  - embryo staging noise.
- **Missing controls:** a node-level time input `κ_i(t)` with matched parameters, and *random prior with dynamic gates*. Without them, "dynamic beats static" (H2) can be explained purely by extra temporal capacity.

**I11 – Chromatin anchor** · I
- **For:** an independent modality, and data exist: GSE205117 (5 times), scTIE (3 times), Fleck (11 times).
- **Against:**
  - Motif accessibility is a TF-level readout. It anchors the regulator side j of m, not edge ij. Edge-level anchoring needs peak-to-gene enhancer accessibility per edge.
  - Chromatin priming leads expression, so a same-time correlation is misspecified.
  - **Circularity:** the real prior comes from SCENIC+/CellOracle, which are built from motifs plus ATAC [S3], and is then validated against ATAC [P H3].
  - Using the anchor as a training penalty and as held-out evidence requires disjoint data.
- **Identifiability:** it helps break the w·m confound and the collinear-regulator confound, but only at node level.

**I12 – KO propagation timing** · I, C
- **For:** genuine interventional temporal information.
- **Against:**
  - Delays are confounded with mRNA/protein decay of the knocked-out TF, KO efficiency and escaper cells, not just edge gating.
  - **Mislabel:** RAF1:ER is a fixed KO plus a stimulus time course, not KO-propagation timing. CD4 is constitutive CRISPRi plus stimulation.
  - Only RENGE is true time-after-KO: about 25,293 cells / (24 conditions × 4 times) ≈ 260 cells per group if evenly split (UNVERIFIED).
  - RENGE (the method) already models multi-step propagation of KO effects through the GRN over time [ext, UNVERIFIED]. It is a direct competitor that is absent from [N].
- **Identifiability:** path delays are resolvable only at the sampling resolution.

**I13 – Binding support** · I (support only)
- **Against:** the CUT&RUN atlas covers 7 TFs at E10.5/E11.5, outside the E6.5–E8.5 gastrulation window. ChIP-Atlas is dominated by cell lines. Binding is not regulation.

**I14 – Mechanism-change interventions** · G (counterfactual populations), I
- **For:** a structural clamp transfers to unseen KOs without needing an embedding, *if the gates do not depend on KO identity*.
- **Against:**
  - In standardised space, `x_k = 0` means mean expression; a KO must be clamped to `−μ_k/σ_k`.
  - CRISPRi is a knockdown, not a clamp, and an instant clamp ignores decay.
  - Gates that depend on c are undefined for an unseen c ([R] warns about categorical embeddings).
  - A KO'd TF with no prior out-edges is predicted to have zero effect.
  - Systema-type generic responses can dominate the metrics.
- **Identifiability:** interventions identify edges in principle (Zweig, stationary case); the time-varying case is open.

**I15 – Within-group coupling** · G
- **For:** correct; [P §5] says cross-condition coupling was an error already made in the first prototype.
- **Against:** small groups (about 260 cells in RENGE) make minibatch OT noisy.

**I16 – Control battery** · validity
- **For:** stronger than the literature (FreeFM, degree- and sign-preserving random prior).

## 3. Internal contradictions and tensions

1. **The gate as specified is not the gate the novelty claim rests on.** [P] and [N] claim per-edge gates "variable in time/state, m_ij(t,x)" and position the work against PFM/Navigo/DynaVelo on "explicit, identifiable state dependence". [S3] implements `m(t,c)` with no x. With x, the gate becomes Jacobian-like; without it, there is no state-specific rewiring.
2. **Terminal forecast is required, but interior stages are tested.** [P §3, §10] says only held-out *terminal* times count as forecast tests, to defeat replay. NX's primaries (Veres d4 from d3; E8.0 from E7.75) are interior removals.
3. **The gates absorb all non-autonomy (I10)**, while the main datasets have batch structure per stage and external stimuli. Shuffled time does not control for this.
4. **The prior is built from ATAC and then validated against ATAC** (I11 vs [S3] prior sources).
5. **Datasets mislabelled as "time after perturbation".** [P §2 point 2] and [P §3.3b] treat RAF1:ER (and CD4, by listing) this way; [L] itself describes RAF1:ER as "fixed KO, stimulus over time". Only RENGE qualifies, and it is small.
6. **Growth is an interpretable output, but the pipeline caps cells per stage**, and [M] warns that cell counts do not measure proliferation.
7. **Context-dependent gates vs zero-shot interventions and contexts.** A gate that depends on c cannot be evaluated for an unseen c; [R]'s own warning about embeddings applies.
8. **Edge-level "when" claims vs rank 2–4 gates and 3–4 timepoints.** Within-interval switch timing comes from the regulariser.
9. **Clamping to 0 in standardised space** contradicts the KO semantics.
10. **Scoop risk vs sequencing.** [N] says "the window is closing" and [P] targets H1+H2 on synthetic and RENGE within 9 months. Yet Phase 3 is deferred [S3] and the next cycle goes to stage-2 horizon diagnostics on the unstructured engine [NX].
11. **No like-for-like comparison.** The real-data positive (EB) used v0.1.10 with stage 2 on. Track 2 gates run with stage 2 off [NX], so the gated model will not be compared against the engine that produced the positive.
12. **H2's "high-rewiring transitions" are undefined on real data**, because the only rewiring sequences (Dileo SCENIC bins) failed the audit.
13. **The planned decay model is not biophysical.** [P] presents its drift as biophysical, but in [S3] decay acts on standardised values, so the parameters lose their kinetic meaning.

## 1. Technical summary

### 1.1 Model
**Core module**
- State: $s\in\{-1,1\}^N$.
- Couplings: off-diagonal $J_{ij}$ is 0 with probability $1-\rho$, otherwise $\mathcal N(0,1/(\rho N))$. $J_{ij}$ and $J_{ji}$ are independent, so the network is asymmetric.
- Self-coupling: uniform, $J_{ii}=J_D\ge 0$.
- Synchronous dynamics:
$$s_i\leftarrow\mathrm{sgn}\Big(\sum_{j\ne i}J_{ij}s_j+J_D s_i\Big).$$
- Fixed point: $s_ih_i+J_D>0\ \forall i$, with $h_i=\sum_{j\neq i}J_{ij}s_j$. The local field has unit variance, so $J_D$ is measured in units of the field's standard deviation. Self-coupling lets a unit keep its sign against a contrary field of up to $J_D$.
- There is no Lyapunov function. At $J_D=0$ the network is chaotic and has no fixed points.

**Multilayer chain** ($L$ modules, independent $J^{(\ell)}$, excitatory coupling $\lambda$ between the same unit in neighbouring modules):
$$s^{(\ell)}_i\leftarrow\mathrm{sgn}\Big(\sum_{j\ne i}J^{(\ell)}_{ij}s^{(\ell)}_j+J_Ds^{(\ell)}_i+\lambda\big(s^{(\ell-1)}_i+s^{(\ell+1)}_i\big)\Big),\qquad s^{(0)}=s^{(L+1)}\equiv0.$$

**With task fields**
$$s_i\leftarrow\mathrm{sgn}\big(\sum_j J_{ij}s_j+\lambda_x\sum_k W^{in}_{ik}x_k+\lambda_y\sum_c W^{back}_{ic}y_c\big)$$
The readout is $\hat y=W^{out}s^*$.

### 1.2 Theory of fixed points
**How many fixed points there are**
- For $L=1$, the annealed count is $\mathbb E\mathcal N_{fp}=(2H(-J_D))^N$, where $H(-x)=\Phi(x)$. A second-moment check shows this is tight and self-averaging.
- So $S_{fp}=\ln2+\ln\Phi(J_D)$.
- For $L=2$: $S_{fp}=\tfrac{\ln2}{2}+\tfrac12\ln[H(-J_D-\lambda)^2+H(-J_D+\lambda)^2]$. It is 0 at $J_D=\lambda=0$ and tends to $\ln 2$ as $J_D,\lambda\to\infty$.
- Values I computed from the $L=1$ formula:

| $J_D$ | $S_{fp}$ (nats per unit) |
|---|---|
| 0.07 | 0.054 |
| 0.35 | 0.242 |
| 0.5 | 0.324 |
| 0.8 | 0.455 |
| 0.95 | 0.506 |

  Fixed points are therefore exponentially many but still an exponentially small fraction, $\Phi(J_D)^N$, of all configurations.

**Typical fixed points are unreachable**
- A replica computation shows that typical fixed points are isolated.
- By the overlap-gap property (Gamarnik), stable algorithms, including the network's own dynamics, cannot reach them in sub-exponential time.

**Local-entropy analysis**
- Definitions: $\mathcal E(\tilde s,d)=-\frac1N\ln\mathcal N(\tilde s,d)$, $Z(d,y)=\sum_{\tilde s}e^{-yN\mathcal E}$, and $\Phi(d,y)=-\lim\frac1{Ny}\ln Z$.
- Internal entropy is $S_I=-\partial_y[y\Phi]$. External entropy is $S_E=-y(\Phi+S_I)$.
- The calculation uses the replica-symmetric ansatz. It is formally a one-step replica-symmetry-breaking calculation with Parisi parameter $m=y$ and region diameter $d=(1-q_1)/2$.
- Criterion for an accessible dense cluster: at $y^*(d)$, where $S_E=0$, $S_I$ must increase monotonically with $d$.

**Thresholds**

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Onset of the dense cluster, core module | $J_{Dc}\simeq0.07$ |
| Onset for $L=2$ with $J_D=0$ | $\lambda_c\lesssim0.45$ |
| Belief propagation with reinforcement first finds fixed points | $J_D\approx0.35$ |
| Likelihood optimisation first finds fixed points | $J_D\approx0.5$ |
| Plain synchronous dynamics converges | $J_D\gtrsim0.8$ |
| Trivial bound (above it every state is already stable) | $J_D\sim\sqrt{2\ln N}$, about 3.7 at $N=1000$ |

The dense, connected, accessible cluster of fixed points is what the paper calls the representation manifold (RM). Its robustness: flipping a large fraction of units sends the dynamics to a fixed point only a few percent away in Hamming distance.

### 1.3 Learning protocol (supplement, Algorithm 1)
1. Start from $s=0$ and run warm-up steps with $\lambda_y=0$ ($L$ steps for $L$ layers).
2. Switch the label field on ($\lambda_y>0$) and relax to a fixed point $s'$.
3. Switch it off again and relax to $s^*$.
4. Apply a margin perceptron/Hebbian update:
$$J_{ij}\leftarrow J_{ij}+\eta\,s^*_is^*_j\,\mathbb 1(s^*_ih_i\le\kappa)\,\mathbb 1(J_{ij}\neq0),\qquad h_i=\sum_jJ_{ij}s^*_j+\lambda_x(W^{in}x)_i.$$
   - $W^{in}$ is updated with the same rule.
   - $J_D$ and $\lambda$ are not updated.
   - Mini-batches average the updates.

What is "new" here: the rule is not contrastive. It does not compare $s'$ with a free-phase state, as equilibrium propagation does. It only stabilises the state the network reached.

Details that matter, from supplement section D:
- $W^{back}$ is never trained ($\eta_{W^{back}}=0$). It works as fixed random class prototypes. The authors report that training it "severely degraded performance".
- In the benchmarks the readout $W^{out}$ is trained with **cross-entropy** in the second half of the epochs. So the reported pipeline is not gradient-free end to end.
- Inference: relax freely from 0 with the input only, then read out.
- Convergence takes 5–10 update steps after a few learning steps.
- The multilayer chain develops limit cycles under synchronous updates. The fix is to update each unit with probability $p$, which is stochastic in practice.

### 1.4 Evidence
**Benchmarks (Fig. 2; plot only, no table in the text)**
- Datasets: MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, Entangled-MNIST (a random projection of MNIST to $D=100$, then binarised), and Tiny-ImageNet features from a fine-tuned ResNet18 ($D=512$, $C=200$; the backbone itself scores 0.765).
- Their model: $N=1000$–$6000$, densities $\rho_J=0.01$, $\rho_{W^{in}}=0.1$.
- Baseline: a **binarised** two-hidden-layer MLP trained with straight-through gradients, widths 109–626, matched parameter count.
- 20 epochs, 5 runs. The verdict is "comparable".

**Hyperparameters**

| Dataset | $J_D$ | $\lambda_x$ | $\kappa$ | $\lambda_y$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MNIST | 0.95 | 5 | 1.78 | 1.62 → 0.8, retuned per width |
| Fashion-MNIST | 0.98 | 4.91 | 1.74 | retuned per width |
| Entangled-MNIST / Tiny-ImageNet | 0.90 | 5 | — | retuned per width |

**Effect of $J_D$**
- Table VII ($N=1600$, Entangled-MNIST): the best setting, $J_D=0.5$, $\lambda_x=5$, $\lambda_y=0.9$, gives train 91% and validation 87%.
- Over training, the overlap between $s'$ and $s^*$ rises 0.930 → 0.991 (epoch 1) → 0.996 (epoch 20).
- **At $J_D=0$ accuracy is about 10% (chance) even though that overlap reaches 1.000.** Stabilising states alone does not produce learning.
- Fig. 14: performance peaks at intermediate $J_D$. As $J_D\to0$ the cluster collapses. As $J_D\to\infty$ the state is fixed by $\mathrm{sgn}(W^{in}x)$, which is just a random-features model.

**Symmetry**
- Symmetric $J$ variants (sym0/1/2) behave like the asymmetric one: all fail at $J_D=0$, even though symmetric dynamics is convergent there, and all jump at $J_D=0.5$.
- The authors conclude that the geometry of the cluster matters, not just whether the dynamics converges.

**Geometry after training (supplement section G; $N=1600$, train 88.54%, validation 85.92%)**
- Path curvature between attractors drops from 1.367 ± 0.027 to 1.222 ± 0.055.
- Displacement after random flips is under 5% before training and smaller after.
- Internal representations cluster by label (Fig. 15).

**Other results**
- Hetero-association capacity grows linearly in $N$, with a slope that increases with depth (up to $L=7$). Overlap with the target grows layer by layer.
- The learned features beat random features and reservoir computing, most clearly at small $N$.

### 1.5 Limitations
**Stated by the authors**
- Binary units in discrete time; continuous time rests only on "preliminary simulations".
- Analytics limited to $L\le2$, under the replica-symmetric ansatz.
- Depth needs inductive biases in the connectivity, left to future work.

**Mine**
1. The headline claim rests on a plot against a deliberately handicapped baseline: a binarised MLP, with no float MLP and no other local learning rules (equilibrium propagation, forward-forward, predictive coding). The tasks are MNIST-scale, and Tiny-ImageNet sits on frozen backprop features.
2. The theory describes **untrained random $J$ with no input**. Learning runs with $\lambda_x=5$ against internal fields of unit standard deviation, so the dynamics is likely input-dominated. That is my inference: the scale of $W^{in}$ is not stated.
   - The optimum $J_D=0.5$ sits below the 0.8 free-convergence threshold.
   - The analytic onset (0.07) is about 10× below the dynamic one (0.8).
   - Nothing predicts how plasticity reshapes the cluster beyond the empirical geometry.
3. The rule is a Gardner-style margin perceptron. The novelty is the protocol and the geometry, not the rule.
4. The theory is deterministic at zero temperature. Noise enters only as a fix for limit cycles.

## 2. What transfers to cell-population dynamics, and how

**Structural fact.** The Phase-3 RA-FM drift (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-18-rafm-phase3-gated-grn-design.md`) is a gated, asymmetric recurrent network in continuous time:
$$v_i=\mathrm{sp}(\gamma_i)\,\mathrm{sp}\big(\beta_i+\textstyle\sum_jS_{ji}\sigma_{ji}\mathrm{sp}(w_{ji})m_{ji}(t,c)\psi_j(x_j)\big)-\mathrm{sp}(\lambda_i)x_i+\kappa_i$$
Its continuous analogue in the Bocconi line of work (thesis; Stern et al. 2014) is $\dot x_i=-x_i+s\,\phi(x_i)+g\sum_jJ_{ij}\phi(x_j)$. Units become bistable for $s>1$, and there are exponentially many stable states. So the paper is about the same model family as the user's drift: sign nonlinearity, no decay, discrete time.

The correspondences:
- $J_D$ ↔ positive autoregulation, the diagonal of the support. This is the classic bistability motif of lineage master regulators.
- $\lambda_x W^{in}x$ ↔ condition and signalling inputs.
- $\lambda_yW^{back}y$ ↔ a transient intervention (induction or knockout pulse).

**T1. Inductive bias: a separate, non-negative self-coupling $a_i$ (optionally gated, $a_i(t)$) per gene, inside the saturating production term.**
- The paper suggests a dimensionless control parameter: $r_i=a_i/\mathrm{sd}_{cells}(\sum_{j\ne i}\text{input}_{ij})$.
  - $r\approx0$: no accessible stable states.
  - $r\sim0.5$–$1$: dense, accessible multistability.
  - $r\gtrsim\sqrt{2\ln N}$ (about 3.8 for 1,200 genes): every state is stable, which is exactly the **persistence** control. The persistence baseline is the $J_D\to\infty$ limit.
- This gives an interpretable "commitment index" per gene and time. Testable claim: $r_i(t)$ of lineage transcription factors rises over time. Cross-check against self-binding motifs in each gene's own enhancers from ATAC data.
- Caveat on identifiability: self-activation and decay are both diagonal terms and can only be separated through the nonlinearity. Marginals alone will not do it. Knockout or induction data will be needed.
- First check whether the CollecTRI-based support even allows an $i\to i$ edge.

**T2. A diagnostic lens on the learned drift. This is the strongest transfer, and it needs no new training.**
Since v0.1.4 (`clock="duration"`) the field $v(x,\text{cond})$ is autonomous, so fixed points and Jacobians are well defined. Adapting supplement sections G and H:
- **(a) Relaxation census.** Integrate the field from every observed cell for a horizon well beyond the biological span (5–10× the largest Δt). Then:
  - cluster the endpoints;
  - count the fraction that converge ($\|v\|<\epsilon$), cycle, or diverge;
  - compare endpoints with the annotated late-stage clusters (ARI);
  - controls: shuffled time and degree-preserving `grn_random_prior`.
- **(b) Robustness, the analogue of their attractor displacement vs noise.** Add dropout or Gaussian noise at fraction $p$ to late-stage cells, relax, and measure displacement relative to the distances between clusters. This gives a "canalisation" curve per cell type.
- **(c) Transition corridors, the analogue of their curvature $\kappa_{\mu\nu}$.** Interpolate between progenitor and fate cells, relax the intermediate states, and measure curvature and which genes flip first. This yields candidate driver genes; compare with Pusuluri et al.'s 1D reaction coordinate.
- **(d) Bifurcations in time.** With gates, $t$ enters only through $m(t)$. Track the fixed points and their Jacobian spectra as functions of $t$. Saddle-node times are then commitment times, and the slow-mode loadings show which regulators control the exit. This answers "which regulators drive which transitions when" directly.
- The analytic $S_{fp}(J_D,\lambda)$ is only a qualitative null. It assumes iid Gaussian $J$, and real GRNs have heavy-tailed hubs, so the central-limit assumption fails.

**T3. Regulariser from the "nudge, then consolidate" rule.**
- The continuous analogue of the margin rule $s_ih_i\ge\kappa$ is a stability hinge on **biologically terminal** cells only:
$$\mathcal L_{stab}=\mathbb E_{x\in\text{terminal}}\big[\|v(x)\|^2+\mathrm{relu}(\lambda_{max}(\mathrm{sym}\,\partial_xv(x))+\kappa)\big]$$
  Compute $\lambda_{max}$ by power iteration or a Hutchinson estimator.
- Hypothesis: it reduces over-dispersion. Per project memory (not re-checked against `outputs/`):
  - On gastrulation E8.0 the path variance is 1.54× the truth and v0.1.10 lost to mean interpolation (2.030 vs 1.513). There the path, not the network, is the bottleneck, so a field-side penalty can only act through the marginal-matching stage.
  - Stage-1 variance ratio is 2.08 on long folds.
- Risk: it freezes cells that are still differentiating. Control: regularise a random half of the terminal cells and evaluate on the other half.
- Adopt it only under the existing rule (≥3/4 pairs better and a negative mean on fresh folds).

**T4. Interventions as transient fields, plus a hysteresis test.**
- Lang 2014 and Szedlak 2014 already model reprogramming and drug control as local fields $h$ on targets. The paper adds the key property: in the dense-cluster regime the state **persists after the field is removed**.
- A fitted drift therefore predicts the population after washout or a pulse. This can be falsified with induction or knockout time courses (e.g. RAF1:ER GSE250559).
- It is interventional evidence that fitting marginals cannot give, and it serves the "positive proof (3)" criterion.

**T5. Coupling between modules ↔ coupling between cells (speculative).**
- With $J_D=0$, homotypic excitatory coupling $\lambda\gtrsim0.45$ alone creates the dense cluster.
- Population reading: a mean-field term where gene $i$'s drift depends on the population mean of gene $i$ (community effect, paracrine positive feedback) can stabilise states the cell-autonomous GRN cannot.
- Cheap add-on: feed moments of the current marginal into the drift (McKean–Vlasov form). With snapshot data this is confounded with time.

**T6. On symmetry.** Since symmetry did not matter but diagonal excitation did, there is no need to force a gradient or potential drift (PRESCIENT-style) for the sake of attractor interpretability. Analyse an asymmetric drift's attractors directly (T2). A landscape "height" would still need a quasi-potential, which the paper does not provide.

**Nuance on "attractor = cell type".** The dense cluster is a near-continuum of marginally separated fixed points, a wide flat region rather than discrete Waddington valleys. Labels only become separated after learning (Fig. 15). The reasonable mapping is cell type ↔ dense high-local-entropy region, with diameter $d$ ↔ heterogeneity within a type, not one fixed point per type. At finite noise such clusters probably merge into one metastable basin.

## 3. What does not transfer, and why
1. **Binary units vs continuous, zero-inflated counts.** The counting relies on sign units and Gaussian fields. For rate models there is only background theory (the thesis, Stern 2014), and neither the dense-cluster result nor learning has been shown there; the thesis does learning in binary networks only.
2. **Deterministic, zero-temperature, synchronous updates.** Gene expression is stochastic. Exponential fixed-point counts are not observable quantities, and at finite noise one needs stationary distributions or quasi-potentials.
3. **No time semantics.** The 5–10 relaxation steps carry no rates or durations, so they do not help goal (2). Nothing in the paper concerns transition timing.
4. **No marginals, no generative modelling.** The model is a deterministic map from input to fixed point used for classification. There are no unpaired snapshots, no growth or death, no density, and no sampling. Goal (1) gets nothing. The neural Langevin machine (Yu, Huang & Huang 2025, cited in the paper; I read only its abstract) is the closer bridge, an asymmetric RNN with Langevin noise used as a generative model.
5. **The supervision signal does not exist for cells.** Clamping labels through random prototypes has no counterpart except clonal fate labels from lineage tracing, which is niche.
6. **The Hebbian update is a correlation rule.** It would learn co-expression, including indirect effects. Lang 2014 admits its $J$ "includes the effect of indirect interactions", and the project's Dileo audit already showed that correlation-type signals mislead. It does not solve causal identifiability. Hidden units here are anonymous, with no unit ↔ gene meaning.
7. **The theory ensemble is iid Gaussian $J$.** GRNs are sparse, signed, hub-dominated and full of motifs, so the thresholds (0.07, 0.35, 0.8, 0.45) do not carry over numerically.
8. **Gradient-free locality is irrelevant here.** The project trains by backprop through the flow-matching loss without trouble; locality only matters for neuro-plausibility.

# Chronocell (Fang, Gorin, Pachter, PLoS Comput Biol 2025): deep read and what it means for the time-resolved generative population model

## 1. Technical summary

**Where it sits in the lab's work.** Several Pachter/Gorin papers lead up to Chronocell:
- **"RNA velocity unraveled"** (Gorin, Fang, Chari, Pachter 2022) criticises RNA velocity:
  - scVelo gives each gene its own latent time per cell, which is incoherent.
  - kNN smoothing and normalisation "nonlinearly transform the molecule distributions" and couple genes that should be independent.
  - It proposes chemical-master-equation (CME) and occupation-measure models instead, with "process time" as the clock of the real process.
- **Monod** (2022) fits steady-state CME models (bursty or constitutive) per pre-clustered cell type. It adds a Poisson technical layer with length-dependent capture, and only rate ratios to burst frequency are identifiable (k=1).
- **biVI** (2023) is scVI with the same steady-state bursty, constitutive or extrinsic decoders. It gives per-cell burst size and γ/k. There is no time.
- **Length bias** (Gorin & Pachter 2022): unspliced capture grows with gene length, through oligo(dT) priming on intronic poly(A) tracts. Unless this is modelled, burst size, γ and β show false length trends.

Chronocell adds the missing piece: a time-dependent (transient) model, at the level of mean counts, with a latent process time.

**Generative model.**
- **Latent per cell:** z_i = (l_i, t_i).
  - Lineage l ∈ {1..L} has a uniform prior; in code the log-prior is −log L − log m.
  - Process time t ∈ (0,1] has a fixed uniform prior, discretised on a regular grid of m points. The code default is m=101.
- **Topology:** given by the user as a 2D array, one row per lineage, listing a path of states s(l,0..K−1). A shared root makes a tree; setting the first state equal to the last makes a loop (used for the cell cycle).
- **Switching times:** 0=τ_0<τ_1<…<τ_K=1, shared by all genes in the default "synchronized" model. A "desynchronized" variant gives each gene its own τ, with an L2 penalty to a prior τ; `fit_tau` refines τ.
- **Per gene j:** one transcription level α_{j,s} per state, and constant β_j (splicing) and γ_j (degradation). The transcription rate is piecewise constant: A_{jl}(t) = α_{j,s(l,k)} for t in interval k.
- **Mean dynamics:** dλ_u/dt = A_{jl}(t) − β_j λ_u and dλ_s/dt = β_j λ_u − γ_j λ_s.
  - Initial condition: the root state at steady state.
  - The solution is in closed form as sums of exponentials over the intervals.
  - Code convention: α is stored as the steady-state unspliced mean, with λ_s(0) = (β/γ)α.
- **Observation:** x_{ijc} ~ Poisson(r_i λ_{jc}(t_i; l_i)), with c ∈ {u, s}. This comes from binomial capture with cell-specific depth r_i.
  - r_i is estimated from the total UMIs of "near-Poissonian" genes: var < 1.2(μ+ξμ²) and μ > 0.01, where ξ is the CV² of read depth. This is iterated about twice.
  - Genes are conditionally independent given z.
- **Degenerate case:** if transitions vanish and cells sit at state steady states, the model becomes a Poisson mixture. This is the sense in which it "interpolates" between clustering and trajectory inference. `mixtures.py` also has `PoissonMixtureSS`, `PoissonMixture` and `GammaPoissonMixture`.

**Identifiability argument.**
- The Poisson family is identifiable and the parametric mean curve is identifiable, so the model is identifiable once p(z) is fixed.
- If p(z) is free, scaling α, β and γ by c while compressing time gives the same marginal likelihood. Fixing the uniform prior on (0,1] is what breaks this.
- For variable genes all parameters are identifiable. For non-variable genes only α and β/γ are.
- Rates come out in units of "process duration". To get hours you rescale afterwards; for example, if the cycle is 24 h, divide β and γ by 24.
- This is identifiability of the population model under a known mixing measure. It says nothing about EM finding the optimum.

**Inference (EM over a grid).**
- **E-step:** Q_i(l,t) = softmax(logL/β_anneal + log prior). The `prior` argument broadcasts to shape (n, L, m), so per-cell priors are possible in the code. This is the hook for capture time.
- **M-step:** each gene separately, with L-BFGS-B and an analytic Jacobian. Bounds: α ∈ [0, bnd], β and γ ∈ [1/bnd, bnd]. There is an optional smoothness penalty λ_a·Σ(α_child−α_parent)², and genes run in parallel through multiprocessing.
- **Starts:** the paper uses 100 random initialisations × 100 steps; the code defaults to n_init=10 and epoch=100, optionally permuting the state order. A warm start from clusters is also available.
- **Model selection:** AIC/BIC computed on the lower bound with k = theta.size. Trajectory and cluster models are compared per gene, and genes are kept only if their trajectory likelihood is higher than their cluster likelihood.
- **Stability diagnostics:**
  - "Average precision" (AP): a random start counts as correct if its mean process time has Pearson r > 0.8 with the best start.
  - 100 bootstrap resamples.

**Gene filter:** mean > 0.02 (u) and > 0.1 (s); u/s ratio > e⁻⁴; variance > 1.2 × the baseline variance.

**Results.**
- **Simulation:** 200 genes, 2000 cells; β ~ logN(2,0.5), γ ~ logN(0.5,0.5), α ~ logN(2,1), r ~ Beta(μ=14, v=164). For a bifurcation (10,000 cells, 100 genes), process-time posterior-mean RMSE is about 0.05.
- **Failure regimes in simulation:**
  - read-depth CV² > 0.1;
  - mean γ outside [1,10] in process units (for example a >10 h process with 30 min half-lives);
  - a non-uniform true sampling distribution: "even … with entropy 4, the errors are big enough that the results are completely wrong";
  - gene-wise gamma noise with CV²=1, which still fits but breaks model selection.
- **Benchmarks:** on Chronocell-simulated data, Slingshot, Monocle 3, DPT and veloVI fail to recover the true time. On dyngen simulations, all methods get the trend but not the true time.
- **PBMC T cells (negative control):** AP = 0.28. AIC wrongly preferred the trajectory model, "likely due to unaccounted noise", so the verdict rests on AP and not on AIC.
- **Forebrain glutamatergic neurons:** unstable across starts; cells pile up near τ_0 with low entropy.
- **Erythroid lineage, multi-timepoint mouse gastrulation** (reference [34]; probably the Pijuan-Sala 2019 subset shipped with scVelo, not confirmed in the text):
  - AP = 0.79, and AIC has a clear minimum in the correct direction.
  - 24 of 49 genes kept.
  - Real time is used only for validation. Posteriors do not differ until E7.5, then progress roughly in step with embryonic day.
- **RPE1 cell cycle, scEU-seq:**
  - Loop topology with 3 intervals (≈ G2/M, G1/S, S/G2).
  - 84 of 182 genes kept.
  - Fitted γ shows "moderate" concordance (CCC) with Battich and TimeLapse-seq half-lives, about as good as Battich-vs-Schofield agreement. The exact coefficients were not extracted.
- **Runtime:** none reported.

**Code size:**
- `inference.py`: about 490–550 lines.
- `models/two_species_ss.py`: 456 lines. `models/one_species.py`: 334 lines; it implements a spliced-only variant that the paper does not analyse.
- `two_species_ss_tau.py` and `plotting.py` exist too.
- BSD-2 licence, pip install "coming soon". The paper's frozen version is pachterlab/FGP_2024 (notebooks: forebrain, erythroid, rpe1, PBMC, Neuron).

## 2. Strengths and limitations

**Strengths**
- **Honest clock.** The latent is a real (relative) time with a generative meaning, not a pseudotime ordering. Genes share one t per cell, which fixes scVelo's per-gene-latent-time incoherence.
- **Counts, not transformed data.** The likelihood is on raw UMIs with an explicit capture/depth model. There is no normalisation, log or kNN smoothing.
- **Interpretable parameters.** α per state, β and γ, switching times τ, lineage posteriors.
- **External validation.** γ was checked against metabolic-labelling half-lives, a rare test of whether the parameters mean something physically.
- **Self-diagnosis.** AP across starts, bootstrap, and the comparison to a cluster model. The paper openly reports negative datasets (PBMC, forebrain).

**Limitations**
- **The time prior.** The fixed uniform prior on (0,1] is both what makes the model identifiable and its most fragile assumption; entropy 4 already breaks it. Time is in process units, and snapshot sampling times are never used in fitting.
- **Topology is given by the user.** Topology search means rerunning and comparing AIC, which is unreliable under Poisson misspecification.
- **Crude transcription model.** Transcription is piecewise constant, switches synchronously across genes, and β and γ are constant in time.
- **No noise beyond Poisson.** No bursting and no extra biological overdispersion, so the likelihood is underdispersed.
- **No regulation.** Genes are independent given z, so there is nothing about which regulators drive which transition.
- **Unspliced counts required** (two-species model), so the length-bias and internal-priming artefacts apply.
- **No population dynamics.** No proliferation, death or influx, and no population sizes.
- **Scale and optimisation.**
  - Small gene sets: 24–100 genes are kept in the real datasets.
  - EM has local optima, hence the 100 restarts.
  - No runtime is reported.
  - About half the candidate genes are rejected by model selection.

## 3. What transfers to the goal (generative, quasi-continuous in time, interpretable)

**The core problem to solve.** We have snapshots at real times T_1..T_n. Within each snapshot, cells are out of sync. We want p(x | T) for any T, with parameters that mean something.

The current engine, cfm_grn_v2, learns a free drift in standardised gene space and is scored with energy distance (ED). The project has already shown three structural weaknesses (source: `memory/phd-project-direction.md`):
- **Linear in the data.** The mean of the spline path equals the spline through the snapshot means for any coupling. Even the best affine combination of snapshot means leaves ED ≥ 0.23 on the simulator.
- **Rare-gene spikes.** 33% (gastrulation) and 41% (embryoid bodies, EB) of selected genes were detected in <1% of cells, giving |z| up to 263,821 (Ptf1a) after standardisation.
- **Representation-dependent wins.**
  - EB: flow 0.267 vs mean interpolation 0.371 in gene space, but a tie in PCA-50.
  - Gastrulation E8.0: a loss, 2.030 vs 1.513. The path itself is over-dispersed (variance 1.54× truth), so the cause is the path, not the network.

**What carries over, mapped to these failures:**
- **Count likelihood on raw UMIs instead of ED on z-scores.** A gene with mean ≈0 is a harmless Poisson/NB term, not a spike. Held-out log-likelihood is a proper scoring rule and does not depend on the representation. Keep ED as a secondary metric only.
- **Shared latent clock and fitted variance.** Predicted spread comes from the mixture over t plus an explicit noise model, not from the geometry of OT-chained splines. This addresses the 1.54× over-dispersion.
- **Nonlinear interpolation.** The prediction at a held-out T is ∫ λ(t) p(t|T) dt with fitted kinetics. That is nonlinear in the data, so it can break the "linear in snapshot means" ceiling.
- **Rates with units.** β and γ, plus switching times, are directly interpretable. γ is externally checkable against half-life atlases.

**Proposed approach (my design, not in the paper): anchor process time to real time with an age-structured population.**

1. **The uniform prior is a special case.** It is exactly the stationary solution of a McKendrick–von Foerster age equation (∂_T n + ∂_t n = g(t) n, with influx n(0,T) = ρ(T)) when inflow is constant, there is no growth, and cells exit at age 1. For a time course, use the time-dependent solution: p(t | T_i) ∝ ρ(T_i − t) · exp(∫₀ᵗ g) · 1[0 ≤ t], plus Chronocell's own point mass at t=0 for a progenitor reservoir.
   - t is the time in hours since entering the process.
   - ρ is the commitment (influx) rate over real time.
   - g is the net growth rate per state.
   - In code, this is just a per-cell `prior` of shape (n, L, m).
2. **Two clocks.** Asynchrony within a snapshot gives the internal ordering (Chronocell's signal). The shift of the age distribution between snapshots calibrates it to hours. This breaks the α/β/γ×c scale ambiguity without the uniform assumption that fails at entropy 4. Branch probabilities π_l(s) may depend on entry time.
3. **Interpretable transcription.** Replace the piecewise-constant α with log α_j(t,l) = b_j + Σ_r W_jr a_r(t,l):
   - W is sparse and signed, with support from a regulator→target prior (CollecTRI, as in RA-FM).
   - a_r are low-rank program activities, piecewise constant on a fine grid of about 30–50 bins.
   - The ODE stays linear in α, so λ_u and λ_s keep the exact exponential-sum form.
   - This turns "state means" into "which programs are active when". These are the gates of the user's Phase 3.
4. **Noise.** NB with gene-specific dispersion, which fixes the underdispersion that made AIC wrong on PBMC. Unspliced capture should scale with gene length (Gorin–Pachter length bias). Depth comes from near-Poissonian genes.
5. **Inference.**
   - The E-step is on the (l, t) grid, but the prior conditioned on T_i makes it far less multimodal than an unconstrained uniform prior.
   - The M-step is per gene (embarrassingly parallel) for b, W, β, γ and dispersion. Population parameters (ρ, π, g) come from weighted MLE on the pooled responsibilities.
   - To scale past about 2k genes and 10⁵ cells, amortise q(l,t | x,T) with an encoder (biVI-style). Keep the kinetic decoder exact.
6. **Generation at any T\*.** Sample l ~ π, then t ~ p(t|T\*), then x ~ NB(r·λ(t,l)). This is quasi-continuous through smooth ρ. Beyond the last snapshot it is honest only as far as the prior on ρ allows.
7. **Falsifiable tests.**
   - (a) On its own simulator, recover process time to RMSE ≤ 0.05 (Chronocell's level).
   - (b) On gastrulation erythroid with unspliced layers, leave one stage out. Compare the held-out NB log-likelihood (paired CI, ≥3 seeds) of: uniform prior, T-anchored prior, mean interpolation, OT interpolation, and cfm_grn_v2 v0.1.10.
   - (c) Check whether T-anchored γ in 1/h agrees with mouse half-life atlases (median roughly 7–9 h, from memory) better than uniform-prior γ.
   - (d) On a simulator with known regulatory gates, recover the active regulator→target links.
   - Keep the existing pre-registration, controls and no-network ceiling diagnosis unchanged; that part of the project is right.

## 4. What does not transfer, or is a trap

- **Uniform process-time prior on a time course.** A time course with expanding or contracting compartments is exactly the non-uniform case that "completely" breaks it. Never use it as is.
- **Equating process time with embryonic day.** The erythroid posteriors are flat until E7.5, and onset or lag has to be modelled. Making the prior a delta at T (process time = real time) throws away the within-snapshot asynchrony.
- **Learning p(t) freely without the T anchor.** This brings back the scale non-identifiability. Even with T, influx ρ and growth g are confounded from proportions alone. Fix g, or bring in cell counts or cell-cycle information.
- **Poisson-only likelihood and AIC/BIC on the lower bound** for topology or model choice: it already failed on PBMC. Use held-out log-likelihood on whole held-out stages.
- **Piecewise-constant α with all genes switching together.** This is clustering with transitions; it is not "regulators drive transitions".
- **Requirement for spliced/unspliced counts:**
  - The project's current matrices (Moon EB, Argelaguet GEX, the Pijuan-Sala atlas files) may not contain spliced/unspliced layers. I did not verify this.
  - Re-quantifying from FASTQ is a large download that needs the user's permission.
  - Unspliced counts carry length and priming bias.
  - The spliced-only `one_species` model exists in code but is not analysed in the paper.
- **Scale.** 24–100 genes per fit, 100 restarts, no runtime reported. Do not plan on 1–2k genes with plain EM over 101 grid points × L lineages without amortisation.
- **Copying the code.** The user's rule is original code only, so re-implement; the model is small (about 1k lines).
- **Dynamics without growth.** No growth/death and no gene–gene regulation, so it cannot be the full population model, only the decoder plus the clock.

# State of the art (2018 to Oct 2026): modelling cell populations over time from snapshots

Legend: **G** = can sample cells or populations. **T** = continuous in real time, meaning collection time; "flow-t" means a rescaled time between pairs of timepoints, and "latent-t" means pseudotime or latent time. **I** = the parameters mean something biological. Venues I confirmed by search this run are marked ✓, and the rest come from my own knowledge.

## (a) Optimal transport (OT), flow matching and Schrödinger bridges (SB)

**Waddington-OT** (Schiebinger, Cell 2019)
- G: only partly. It gives couplings between timepoints and can interpolate between them, but it has no sampler for new cells.
- T: anchored to the real collection days, with straight-line (geodesic) interpolation in between.
- I: growth is set from proliferation and apoptosis gene signatures, and ancestor/descendant maps follow from the couplings.
- Evidence: reprogramming data with 39 timepoints; held-out timepoints are compared with the geodesic interpolation.

**Neural ODE / continuous-normalising-flow family**
- TrajectoryNet (ICML 2020), MIOFlow (NeurIPS 2022; works in a geodesic autoencoder latent space), scNODE (Bioinformatics/ISMB 2024; VAE plus latent ODE), TIGON (Nat Mach Intell 2023; dynamic unbalanced OT with growth).
- G: yes. T: real collection times. I: low. TIGON reads a GRN off the Jacobian of the velocity field after training.

**PRESCIENT** (Nat Commun 2021)
- A diffusion driven by a learned scalar potential, with weighting for proliferation. G, T and a potential, so I is partial.

**JKOnet\*** (NeurIPS 2024 oral ✓)
- Learns potential, interaction and internal-energy terms from the first-order optimality conditions of the JKO scheme.
- Closed-form solution when the energy is linear in its parameters; applied to embryoid-body data over 27 days.
- I is high only within the gradient-flow class: rotational (curl) dynamics cannot be represented.

**DeepRUOT** (ICLR 2025 oral ✓)
- Regularised unbalanced OT: stochastic dynamics plus growth and death, learned without priors. Proves a link to the Schrödinger bridge.
- Builds a Waddington landscape; shown on a synthetic GRN, a high-dimensional Gaussian mixture and haematopoiesis.
- G, T, and partial I (potential and growth only).

**Unbalanced, branching and interacting Schrödinger bridges (2025–2026)**
- Branched SB matching, BranchSBM (arXiv 2506.09007; venue not confirmed).
- Unbalanced SB with birth–death jumps (2025).
- "Beyond Continuity", simulation-free discrete branching dynamics (ICML 2026 ✓).
- Unbalanced mean-field SB with cell–cell interactions (2025; venue not confirmed).
- Multi-marginal SB matching, MSBM (2025).
- ARTEMIS, a VAE plus unbalanced diffusion SB (venue not confirmed).
- All are G and T. I is limited to growth fields and branch weights.

**Flow-matching family**
- Action matching (ICML 2023), Wasserstein Lagrangian flows (ICML 2024), OT-CFM (TMLR 2024), [SF]²M (AISTATS 2024), Metric FM (NeurIPS 2024).
- MMFM (ICLR 2025 ✓): spline interpolation across timepoints and conditions plus classifier-free guidance, which fixes irregular time spacing.
- Multi-marginal stochastic flow matching, MMSFM (arXiv 2508.04351).
- Lagrangian FM (arXiv 2605.15419, 2026) and scFM (arXiv 2605.22340, 2026; both interpolation and extrapolation).
- Pairwise CFM runs on flow-t in [0,1] per interval, so querying between timepoints amounts to assuming constant-speed least action.

**Perturbation flow matching**
- CellFlow (Klein, …, Theis, bioRxiv 2025.04.11.648220 ✓): a conditional FM from control to perturbed cells, with OT pairing and pretrained perturbation embeddings.
- G yes. Time is only a condition. I is low.
- Do not confuse it with CellFlux/"CellFlow" for morphology (arXiv 2502.09775).

**Typical failure modes for (a)**
- The least-action prior gives wrong couplings for cycles, converging fates or fast transits.
- Growth and transition are confounded when absolute cell counts are missing.
- Results are judged mostly by interpolation in 5–100 PCA dimensions, and drifts are black boxes.
- Weinreb et al., PNAS 2018 ("fundamental limits"): from snapshots alone the curl part of the dynamics cannot be identified, and potential and diffusion trade off against each other.
- Lavenant et al., Ann Appl Probab 2024 (not confirmed this run): consistency is guaranteed only when the drift is a gradient and the diffusivity is known.

## (b) Mechanistic and biophysical models

**scVelo** (Nat Biotech 2020)
- Rates are relative to the splicing rate (β), and its latent time is not real time.
- Critiques: Bergen, MSB 2021; Gorin, "RNA velocity unraveled", PLOS CB 2022; Zheng, "Pumping the brakes", Genome Biol 2023 (not confirmed this run).
- Problems they raise: the steady-state and constant-rate assumptions, transcriptional boosts, kNN smoothing artefacts, and projecting velocities onto embeddings.

**RegVelo** (Cell 2026, doi 10.1016/j.cell.2026.04.022 ✓)
- A prior GRN gates transcription rates in coupled splicing ODEs, solved with a VAE that infers latent time.
- It simulates in-silico knockouts. In zebrafish neural crest it predicted tfec as an early driver and elf1 as a regulator of pigment fate, both validated by CRISPR knockout and Perturb-seq.
- I is high. T is latent-t. G is only per-cell, not a population with growth.

**Dynamo** (Cell 2022)
- Metabolic labelling gives absolute velocity in real units. Adds an analytic vector field, Jacobians, least-action paths and in-silico perturbation.
- I is high and T is the labelling clock. The field is deterministic per cell, with no population sampler and no growth.

**cell2fate** (Nat Methods 2025, doi 10.1038/s41592-025-02608-3 ✓)
- A linearised velocity ODE fitted in a fully Bayesian way and split into interpretable modules, mapped spatially onto developing brain.
- Latent-t.

**Chronocell** (PLOS CB 2025 ✓)
- Introduces "process time": an identifiable biophysical model that moves smoothly between a continuous trajectory and discrete clusters.
- Covers small gene sets. G (count model) and I; T is process-t, not real time.

**Monod / biVI** (Carilli, Nat Methods 2024, not confirmed)
- Chemical-master-equation burst kinetics; a generative count model with no time axis.

**Metabolic labelling assays**
- sci-fate (Nat Biotech 2020), scEU-seq (Science 2020), scNT-seq (Nat Methods 2020).
- These are the only source of a real-time clock inside each cell (label duration).
- Limits: few datasets, 4sU side effects, low capture, and only short time windows.

## (c) GRN-structured dynamics

**CellOracle** (Nature 2023)
- Per-cluster linear GRNs built from chromatin accessibility; a knockout produces a shift vector on the embedding.
- I yes. Not G and not T. Validated with zebrafish knockouts.

**PHOENIX** (Genome Biol 2024, not confirmed this run)
- A biologically informed neural ODE with Hill-like terms and a prior network, at genome scale. Trained on time series or pseudotime.

**Cell-MNN** (ICLR 2026 ✓, arXiv 2510.02903)
- Encoder–decoder whose latent dynamics are a locally linear ODE with explicit gene–gene interactions.
- Held-out-timepoint W1 (EMD in PCA, exact solver), averaged over datasets: **0.741** versus OT-MFM 0.776 and DeepRUOT 0.846.
- This is the only one here that is roughly G, T and I at the same time.

**StructureFlow** (arXiv 2510.16656, 2025 ✓)
- Builds on [SF]²M plus entropic OT. The learned probability-flow ODE carries the network structure in its parametrisation, and training needs no simulation.
- Tested on synthetic systems, biologically plausible simulations and real data with interventions.
- No growth term. Still a preprint.

**FlowGRN** (arXiv 2608.09798, Aug 2026, technical report ✓)
- Reconstructs trajectories with flow matching and then infers a GRN that is robust to dropout. Post hoc, not a single joint model.

**"GRNvelo"**
- Not found under that name. The closest hit is "Decoding GRNs from single-cell RNA velocity" (arXiv 2608.09722); whether this is the method you meant is unconfirmed.

**Others**
- DynaVelo (bioRxiv 2025.04.24.650458 ✓): multiome velocities plus TF-motif accessibility in germinal-centre B cells; predicts loss-of-function and rescue.
- OTVelo (2024/25): OT-based gene velocities, then a GRN.
- RENGE (Commun Biol 2023, 6:1290 ✓): time-series CRISPR scRNA-seq; models how a knockout propagates and separates direct from indirect edges.
- CellProphet (bioRxiv 2025.02.05.636766): dynamic GRN for virtual differentiation.
- An interpretable biophysical neural-ODE GRN (bioRxiv 2025.09.19.676870).
- CycleGRN (bioRxiv 2025.11.12.688126).
- scMultiSim (Nat Methods 2025, per this run's search snippet; not otherwise confirmed): a ground-truth simulator with a kinetic or Beta-Poisson mode, GRN, cell–cell interactions and chromatin accessibility.
- **Navigo: not found.**

**Failure modes for (c)**
- GRNs are barely identifiable from observational snapshots; BEELINE (Pratapa, Nat Methods 2020) found AUPRC close to random for many methods.
- Motif or ATAC priors carry many false positives.
- Linear or locally linear models fail at bifurcations.
- Methods built on velocity inherit velocity's errors.
- Validation against perturbations is rare.

## (d) Landscapes, attractors and interpretable latent dynamics

**Potential plus noise SDEs**
- PRESCIENT; DeepRUOT; and PI-SDE (Bioinformatics/ISMB 2024, 40:ii120 ✓). PI-SDE enforces a Hamilton–Jacobi (least-action) equation on the potential and uses separate networks for drift and diffusion.

**Geometric models of cell fate**
- Sáez, Blassberg, Camacho-Aguilar, Siggia, Rand and Briscoe, Cell Systems 2022, 13:12–28 ✓.
- Catastrophe theory plus approximate Bayesian computation fitted to stem-cell fate under combinations of signals. It predicts outcomes and finds two archetypal ways a binary decision is made.
- I is maximal. It is low-dimensional with a hand-chosen form, and its time axis is real (the signal schedule).

**Generative epigenetic landscapes** (bioRxiv 2025.06.09.658705)
- Gradient plus rotational fields built from locally weighted "valley" elements, so the model stays interpretable and does not assume a pure gradient.
- Also related: "Generative modelling of single-cell population time series for differentiation landscapes" (preLights). Authors and venue not confirmed.

**Latent-dynamics models**
- scTour (Genome Biol 2023): VAE plus latent neural ODE.
- LatentVelo (Cell Rep Methods 2023, not confirmed).
- Both use latent-t.

**SINDy and switching dynamical systems**
- I found no established single-cell application. This is a real gap, because both need trajectories rather than snapshots.

**Failure modes for (d)**
- Curl components cannot be identified from snapshots.
- Landscapes are fixed in time (autonomous), so they do not capture time-dependent bifurcations.
- Visual "landscapes" are often drawn in 2D after the fact.

## (e) Lineage-informed models

**Weinreb 2020** (Science)
- LARRY barcodes, days 2/4/6 of haematopoiesis. Fate bias exists before it is visible in transcriptome state, which implies hidden heritable variables.

**CoSpar** (Nat Biotech 2022)
- A coherent, sparse transition map learned from clones observed across timepoints.

**LineageOT** (Nat Commun 2021)
- OT corrected by the lineage tree at the later timepoint.

**moslin** (Genome Biol, Oct 2024, doi 10.1186/s13059-024-03422-4 ✓)
- Fused Gromov–Wasserstein that uses lineage at two or more timepoints and accounts for growth and sampling. Applied to fibroblast states in zebrafish heart regeneration.

**Others**
- Clonotrace (2025, PMC12424799) and CellRank 2 (Nat Methods 2024; real-time OT and labelling kernels).
- These are Markov fate maps: I yes, not G, and T is discrete.

**Failure modes for (e)**
- Clones are subsampled, CRISPR scars recur independently (homoplasy), barcodes are lost (dropout), and few systems have this data.

## (i) Largest open gaps

1. **Identifiability.** No model jointly uses snapshots, a labelling clock, lineage and perturbations in one generative continuous-time model with identifiability guarantees. Without these, curl, diffusion, growth and GRN edges stay confounded, so "interpretable" parameters often cannot be identified.
2. **Real time versus interpretability.** Interpretable kinetic models (RegVelo, cell2fate, Chronocell, scTour) run on latent time. Real-time models (DeepRUOT, TIGON, the flow-matching and SB families) have black-box drifts. Dynamo is the exception but is deterministic and has no population growth.
3. **Structured drift with growth, branching and noise.** StructureFlow and Cell-MNN have structure but no growth or death. RUOT, unbalanced SB and branching SB have growth and branching but MLP drifts, interpreted only after training via Jacobians.
4. **Time-varying regulation and extrapolation.** Almost all evaluations are interpolation. GRN rewiring over time and bifurcation parameters that depend on time are untreated beyond the low-dimensional Sáez/Rand models. Cell–cell interactions only appear in mean-field SB.
5. **Benchmarks.** No real-data benchmark has ground-truth dynamics. There is no calibrated uncertainty, and fidelity in full gene space is rarely tested.

## (ii) Closest existing method to the goal

No single method meets all three requirements.

- **Most complete on paper: StructureFlow** (2025 preprint). It is generative ([SF]²M-type SB), runs on real collection times, and builds the network structure into the drift. It falls short in four ways:
  - balanced mass, so no proliferation or death;
  - entropic-OT couplings impose least action between timepoints;
  - GRN identifiability from snapshots is unproven, and evidence is mostly synthetic;
  - no lineage or labelling anchor.
- **Most mature for generative plus real time: DeepRUOT.** Its interpretability is only a potential and a growth field in a reduced space, with no gene-level regulators.
- **Most biologically validated for interpretability: RegVelo.** It has a GRN plus CRISPR and Perturb-seq validation, but it uses latent time and is not a population-level generative model.
- Cell-MNN is the nearest compromise in benchmark terms, but its locally linear interactions live in a latent space.

## (iii) Evaluation standards and pitfalls

**Standard setup**
- Leave one timepoint out and score the predicted marginal:
  - W1 (EMD, exact POT solver) in PCA, either 5 dimensions (TrajectoryNet tradition) or 100 dimensions ([SF]²M, Metric FM);
  - W2;
  - MMD with RBF kernels at several bandwidths;
  - energy distance.
- Usual datasets: embryoid body (5 timepoints, 0–27 days), the NeurIPS-2022 CITE/Multiome data, Schiebinger reprogramming, Weinreb LARRY, and the Veres pancreas data.

**Pitfalls in interpolation scoring**
- Fitting PCA on all timepoints leaks information from the held-out one.
- 5-dimensional PCA makes the metric saturate.
- MMD depends on the chosen bandwidth.
- Cell counts are unbalanced across timepoints.
- No trivial baselines are reported. The nearest observed timepoint and OT straight-line (McCann) interpolation are often close to deep models.
- Usually a single seed and no confidence intervals.
- Extrapolating the last timepoint is rarely reported.

**Unbalanced models**
- Growth rates are only relative unless absolute cell counts are measured.

**Perturbation tasks**
- Hold out perturbations, not cells.
- Simple linear or mean baselines match or beat deep models (Ahlmann-Eltze et al., Nat Methods 2025; not confirmed this run). Systematic shifts between control and perturbed cells inflate scores (Systema, 2025; not confirmed).

**Lineage**
- Score transition maps against clonal fates, e.g. day-2 progenitor bias against day-4/6 outcomes (Weinreb, CoSpar).
- Pitfalls: clonal subsampling and homoplasy.

**GRN**
- AUPRC is inflated by easy random negatives. The project's own audit (from the memory index in context; I did not re-read the file) found a reported AUPRC of 0.94 drops to about 0.50 under degree-matched negatives.
- Use degree-matched negatives, Perturb-seq or ChIP ground truth, and simulators such as scMultiSim.

**Velocity**
- CBDir and ICCoh are circular when the "known" transitions come from the same annotation used to build the model.

# Theory dossier: what can be identified about cell-population dynamics

Notation: ρ_t is the cell-state distribution at clock time t, b is the drift, D or σ² is the diffusion, g(x) is net growth, A is a linear drift matrix and H = GGᵀ.

## 1. Limit statements

**L1. The velocity behind a deterministic flow is not unique (continuity equation).**
- If v_t reproduces the marginals ρ_t, then so does v_t + w_t for any w_t with ∇·(ρ_t w_t) = 0.
- This holds for any number of timepoints, even a continuum.
- Flow models (CFM, rectified flow, OT-interpolants) therefore *choose* a velocity; they do not estimate it. Optimal transport and Benamou–Brenier pick the unique minimum-kinetic-energy gradient field. Action Matching (Neklyudov et al., ICML 2023) learns exactly that field.
- Consequence: a CFM velocity has no mechanistic meaning unless the model class is restricted (L3, L5, L6).

**L2. A steady-state population fixes almost nothing (Weinreb, Wolock, Tusi, Socolovsky, Klein, PNAS 2018, 115:E2467).**
- Model: ∂ρ/∂t = −∇·(ρv) + D∇²ρ + R(x)ρ with ∂ρ/∂t = 0. Any number of snapshots is consistent with infinitely many dynamics.
- A unique answer needs all three of:
  - (a) no rotational (curl) component, i.e. potential dynamics;
  - (b) known D;
  - (c) known net growth R(x).
- Even then only the potential part is recovered.
- Oscillations such as the cell cycle cannot be seen.
- The measured state must also be Markov-complete. Unmeasured chromatin, protein or epigenetic memory breaks inference.
- Weinreb et al. 2020 (Science, LARRY lineage barcodes) showed that the transcriptome only partly predicts clonal fate, so these hidden variables are real.

**L3. Gradient drift with known diffusion is identifiable (Lavenant, Zhang, Kim, Schiebinger, Ann. Appl. Probab. 2024).**
- Model: dX = −∇Ψ(t,X)dt + √τ dB with τ known.
- The law on paths is fixed by the full curve of marginals. It is the unique minimiser of relative entropy to Brownian motion (diffusivity τ) under those marginals, i.e. a Schrödinger bridge.
- The Global Waddington-OT estimator is consistent as the time grid gets dense, even with few cells per timepoint ("down to 1" is from memory, UNVERIFIED).
- It fails when:
  - the drift is not a gradient (counterexample: rotation around an isotropic stationary Gaussian);
  - τ is unknown;
  - there are only a few widely spaced timepoints. Then the bridge coupling is a prior, not an estimate.

**L4. Algorithmic counterpart (Chizat, Zhang, Heitz, Schiebinger, NeurIPS 2022).**
- Same model class as L3.
- A convex objective (data fit + entropy relative to a reference process) over path measures.
- Mean-field Langevin dynamics in path space, with global convergence guarantees; tolerates noisy observations.
- It adds no identifiability beyond L3.

**L5. Gradient flows: drift and noise are jointly identifiable iff the data are out of equilibrium (Guan et al., AISTATS 2026, PMLR v300).**
- Model: dX = −∇Ψ(X)dt + σdW, with Ψ fixed in time and σ scalar.
- (∇Ψ, σ²) is identifiable from the marginals **iff p₀ is not the stationary distribution.**
- At equilibrium only Ψ/σ² is identified (Boltzmann form, ρ∞ ∝ e^{−2Ψ/σ²}).
- Proof idea: non-identifiability is equivalent to observing a "residual process" at equilibrium.
- Anisotropic or state-dependent noise is not covered (UNVERIFIED).

**L6. Linear drift with additive noise, including non-gradient (curl) parts (Guan, Janssen, Rahmani, Warren, Zhang, Robeva, Schiebinger, arXiv 2410.22729, 2024–25).**
- Model: dX = AXdt + GdW.
- (A, GGᵀ) is identifiable from the marginals **iff X₀ has no generalized rotational symmetry.** With a symmetric (e.g. isotropic) X₀, rotational parts of A cannot be seen.
- The causal graph is the set of non-zero entries of A (cycles allowed). Off-diagonal entries of GGᵀ indicate latent confounding.
- Estimator: APPEX (alternating projection with an anisotropic entropic-OT step); it lowers the KL divergence at each iteration.
- Nonlinear non-gradient drift: no general result found; I treat it as open (UNVERIFIED).
- Earlier work for observed trajectories from a fixed x₀: Wang et al., NeurIPS 2023 (linear SDEs, additive and multiplicative noise).

**L7. If the dynamics may change arbitrarily over time, nothing is identifiable.**
- With b(t,x) unrestricted and D unknown, any smooth marginal curve is reproduced by infinitely many (b_t, D_t), including D = 0.
- Time-homogeneity (or a low-dimensional parametric time dependence) is therefore **the** assumption that makes inference possible.
- The same assumption is the only thing that licenses extrapolation past the last timepoint. Extrapolation is never identified by the data.

**L8. Growth and transport are confounded.**
- Normalised snapshots show ρ_t/mass only.
- In ∂ρ = −∇·(ρv) + (g − ḡ)ρ, any g(x) can be matched by solving an elliptic PDE for v. So g is not identified.
- Unbalanced OT (UOT) and Wasserstein–Fisher–Rao (WFR) methods choose g through the size of the mass-creation penalty, which is a prior. Examples: WOT (Schiebinger et al., Cell 2019), TIGON, DeepRUOT.
- Total cell counts N(t) fix only the mean growth ḡ(t).
- Ventre, Forrow, Gadhiwala, Chakraborty, Angel, Schiebinger (J. Math. Biol. 2026): lineage trees separate proliferation from differentiation. Without death or subsampling this comes with guarantees and no growth prior.

**L9. Time scale: only time rescaling is fixed, not absolute time.**
- Kinetic models (unspliced/spliced) are unchanged under t → ct with all rates divided by c. A static snapshot gives only rate ratios (e.g. γ/β).
- Absolute time needs a clock: the labelling pulse duration, or experimental time plus transient dynamics.
- Pseudotime is defined only up to a monotone reparametrisation, plus a chosen root.
- Process time (Chronocell; Fang, Gorin, Pachter, PLoS Comput Biol 2025) is identifiable inside a model with constant transcription rates per state. On cluster-like data the trajectory model collapses to clustering, and Chronocell can test which case applies.
- Flow time s ∈ [0,1] in CFM/SB is an integration variable. It is biological time only through an assumed mapping between timepoints.

**L10. Direction of time.**
- Reversible (gradient) processes at equilibrium have time-symmetric path laws, so direction cannot be identified even from perfect trajectories.
- Across snapshots, direction comes only from clock labels.
- Within a snapshot, direction needs splicing, metabolic labelling, or chromatin priming (MultiVelo, Nat Biotech 2023).

**L11. The same cell is never observed twice.**
- Measurement destroys the cell, so the joint law of (X_s, X_t) for one cell is never observed.
- It comes either from the model class (L3, L5, L6) or from clonal relatives.
- Relatives are not the cell itself: sisters drift apart after barcoding.
- Path-level non-Markov memory cannot be tested.

**L12. Asynchrony and the cell cycle.**
- A clock-time snapshot mixes cells at different developmental stages. Under a time-homogeneous Markov model this just becomes part of the initial distribution, which is harmless.
- If a hidden global clock exists (embryo stage, morphogen exposure), dynamics are non-Markov in expression space. The fitted diffusion then absorbs the asynchrony.
- The cell cycle is a rotational, non-gradient motion. It breaks the assumptions of L3 and L5 and enters growth through mass doubling.

**L13. Batch and time are confounded.**
- Usually each timepoint is a separate library or batch, so temporal change and batch effect are perfectly confounded.
- Batch correction can erase real temporal signal.
- Only the experimental design can fix this.

**L14. Measurement noise and sample size.**
- The observed marginal is the true marginal blurred by Poisson/negative-binomial sampling noise. Fitted diffusion mixes intrinsic and technical noise unless the count model is explicit.
- Estimating marginals in Wasserstein-2 distance converges at rate n^{−1/d}, so in high dimension the marginals are effectively unknown at finite n.
- Entropic OT reaches n^{−1/2}, but with constants exponential in 1/ε (Genevay et al. 2019; Mena & Niles-Weed 2019).

**L15. GRN edges from dynamics.**
- The Jacobian ∂b_i/∂x_j is identified only along directions in which the data vary (the manifold tangent plus noise spread).
- With intrinsic dimension d ≪ G genes, about G(G−d) Jacobian entries are set by the regulariser, not by the data.
- TFs whose expression is collinear along a 1-D trajectory are interchangeable.
- mRNA is not TF activity. Unmeasured species produce effective, indirect edges.
- Stationary Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) case (Dettling, Homs, Améndola, Drton, Hansen, *Identifiability in continuous Lyapunov models*, arXiv 2209.03835): the drift is globally identifiable from the covariance iff the sparsity graph is simple (no directed 2-cycles), given known volatility.
- L6 removes the need for sparsity by using transient data instead.

**L16. Low-rank activity factors can be rotated freely.**
- If b(x) = U·a(x) with rank k, then (UR)(R⁻¹a) gives the same dynamics for any invertible k×k R. The statement "program j drives transition m" is not identified.
- There is also a column scale and sign symmetry: (u_j·c)(a_j/c) with c = −1 flips the sign.
- Fixes:
  - nonnegativity plus a "sufficiently scattered" condition (NMF uniqueness; Donoho & Stodden 2003; Fu, Huang, Sidiropoulos 2019);
  - anchor genes / sparse decoding (Moran, Sridhar, Wang, Blei 2022);
  - fixed prior TF–target supports with distinct columns, which leaves only per-column scale;
  - a normalisation convention for scale and sign.

**L17. Latent VAEs.**
- Unsupervised nonlinear latents are not identifiable: any measure-preserving diffeomorphism of the latent space fits equally well (Hyvärinen & Pajunen 1999; Locatello et al., ICML 2019).
- iVAE (Khemakhem et al., AISTATS 2020) uses an auxiliary variable u and needs nk+1 distinct u values, where n is the number of sufficient statistics per latent.
  - Gaussian prior with mean and variance modulated: **2k+1** values.
  - Variance only: **k+1** values.
- Clock time can serve as u. With T timepoints that gives k ≤ (T−1)/2 latents (or k ≤ T−1 for variance only). For example, **5 timepoints support only 2 latents** under mean+variance modulation. Perturbation conditions raise the count.
- Other routes:
  - sparsity (Lachapelle et al., CLeaR 2022; Zheng, Ng, Zhang, NeurIPS 2022; Moran 2022);
  - interventions: Squires et al., ICML 2023 (linear, at least one intervention per latent); Zhang, Greenewald, Squires, Srivastava, Shanmugam, Uhler, NeurIPS 2023 (soft interventions, identified up to an equivalence class, tested on Perturb-seq); Lopez et al. 2023 (sVAE+).
- Temporal causal-representation guarantees (TDRL, LEAP, NCTRL) assume paired x_{t−1}, x_t from the same unit. They do **not** carry over to destructive snapshots unless couplings come from lineage.

## 2. Limitation → what resolves it → cost and availability

| Limitation | What resolves it | Cost / availability |
|---|---|---|
| Velocity not unique for flows (L1) | Restrict the model class to SDE + gradient (L3/L5) or linear (L6); local velocity data | Free (assumption) / labelling, see below |
| Drift vs noise mixed at equilibrium (L2, L5) | Out-of-equilibrium time course; metabolic labelling | Time course: one library per timepoint, cheap. Labelling (scSLAM, scNT, sci-fate, scEU-seq) is routine in vitro and rare in vivo |
| Curl / oscillation invisible (L2, L6, L12) | Non-symmetric or multiple initial conditions (L6); labelling velocities (dynamo; Qiu et al., Cell 2022); live imaging of reporters | Design choice, cheap / moderate / high, low throughput |
| Growth vs transport (L8) | Lineage barcodes with clone sizes (LARRY, CellTag, DARLIN, CARLIN, GESTALT); lineage trees (Ventre 2026); proliferation and apoptosis markers; total counts (ḡ only) | Barcodes: public hematopoiesis, reprogramming, zebrafish and mouse datasets. In vivo mouse is costly. Counts are almost free |
| No coupling across time (L3 with finite timepoints, L11) | Clonal barcodes (CoSpar; LineageOT; moslin, Genome Biol 2024 with growth and subsampling) | As above; sisters are only proxies |
| No absolute time scale (L9) | Labelling pulse; dense timepoints | Labelling moderate; extra timepoints cheap per lane |
| Direction inside a snapshot (L10) | Splicing (free, but only rate ratios); labelling; Multiome chromatin priming | Splicing free; 10x Multiome roughly 2× scRNA |
| Hidden variables, non-Markov (L2, L12) | Multiome, CITE-seq protein, spatial; lineage–fate checks | Moderate; many public sets |
| GRN direction, cycles, off-manifold Jacobian (L15) | Perturbations: Perturb-seq; perturbation × time (e.g. Saunders et al. 2023, zebrafish crispants over time, UNVERIFIED details); transient data (L6) | High for in vivo time-resolved perturbations; public K562/RPE1 data have no time axis |
| Factor rotation (L16) | Nonnegativity, anchor genes, prior TF–target masks, per-program interventions | Free (assumptions) / priors from motifs or ChIP |
| Latent non-identifiability (L17) | Auxiliary u (time, condition) with ≥2k+1 distinct values; sparsity; interventions | Limited by number of timepoints and conditions |
| Batch × time (L13) | Hashing or multiplexing, staggered starts, collecting all timepoints in one run | Low cost; must be planned up front |
| Technical vs intrinsic noise (L14) | Explicit negative-binomial/Poisson likelihood; replicates | Free (modelling) |

## 3. Consequences

**Legitimate from snapshots alone (time-labelled, few timepoints):**
- Changes in the marginals: relative cell-type proportions and the appearance of new states.
- Generative sampling *at measured times*.
- Interpolation between timepoints, stated as "consistent with the minimal-action / gradient + Brownian prior". It is not identified with large gaps.
- A gradient landscape and fate probabilities, valid under assumptions (gradient, time-homogeneous, out of equilibrium). By L5 the noise level σ is then learnable rather than fixed.
- A linear(ised) A and H in a low-dimensional space, including curl and graph support, if X₀ is not symmetric (L6).
- Correlational regulator rankings along the fitted flow.
- Leave-one-timepoint-out prediction as the main way to falsify a model.

**Needs extra data:**
- Absolute rates and per-cell developmental time: labelling plus a kinetic model.
- State-specific growth vs differentiation: lineage data plus counts.
- Clonal fate and coupling: barcodes.
- Nonlinear curl or oscillations: labelling or imaging.
- Causal GRN edges, the off-manifold Jacobian, and named, unrotated programs: perturbations or structural priors.
- Removing batch confounding: multiplexed design.

**Never testable from destructive single-cell data:**
- The trajectory of an individual cell, and its counterfactual. Sisters are only proxies.
- Non-Markov memory in unmeasured variables, beyond what the model assumes.
- Extrapolation past the last timepoint. It rests purely on the time-homogeneity assumption (L7).
- Time-inhomogeneous drift with unknown diffusion.
- An additive constant in the potential, and the overall time unit without a clock.

## 4. Positive results to exploit

1. **Guan et al., AISTATS 2026.** Nonlinear gradient drift and σ² are jointly identifiable iff not started at equilibrium. How to use it: differentiation time courses already satisfy this, so σ can be learned rather than fixed; a time-homogeneous Ψ is required.
2. **Guan et al., arXiv 2410.22729.** Linear A (non-gradient allowed) and full H, plus the causal graph, are identifiable iff X₀ is not rotation-symmetric; APPEX estimator. How to use it: a linear or low-rank-linear SDE on program activities is the identifiable "interpretable core". Avoid symmetric starting populations, and use multiple initial conditions or perturbations.
3. **Lavenant et al., AAP 2024.** Gradient + known τ gives an identified path law, with consistency as timepoints get dense. Design rule: prefer **many timepoints with fewer cells** over few timepoints with many cells.
4. **Chizat et al., NeurIPS 2022.** A convex, globally convergent estimator that handles noisy observations.
5. **Maddu, Chardès, Shelley, PNAS 2025 (probability flow inference, PFI).** Separates force from intrinsic noise; for OU processes the regularised problem has a unique solution when distributions are well sampled.
   - **Zhang, Maddu, Qiu, Chardès, NeurIPS 2025** (unbalanced PFI) adds growth. Whether growth is truly identified or only regularised is UNVERIFIED.
6. **Zhang & Stumpf, NeurIPS 2025.** Schrödinger bridge with a multivariate OU (mvOU) reference that allows non-gradient (asymmetric) drift. Closed form for Gaussian marginals; a simulation-free flow-plus-score-matching algorithm (mvOU-OTFM). This plugs directly into a flow-matching engine.
7. **Shen, Berlinghieri, Broderick, AISTATS 2025.** Learns the reference SDE across multiple snapshots by iterative refinement. "Oh SnapMMD!" (arXiv 2505.16082) covers forecasting past the last snapshot (content UNVERIFIED).
8. **Ventre et al., J. Math. Biol. 2026** (lineage trees separate proliferation from differentiation) and **moslin, Genome Biol 2024** (lineage-plus-expression coupling across timepoints with growth and subsampling).
9. **Dettling et al., Lyapunov models.** Edges are identifiable from a single equilibrium snapshot iff the graph has no 2-cycles and the volatility is known.
10. **Chronocell, PLoS Comput Biol 2025.** Identifiable process time, plus a test of whether a dataset is trajectory-like or cluster-like.
11. **iVAE with time or condition as auxiliary variable**, sparse decoding, and soft-intervention disentanglement (Zhang et al., NeurIPS 2023). These are the routes to named latent programs.

**Recommended design, synthesised from 1–11:** a time-homogeneous SDE in program space,
dZ = (−∇Ψ(Z) + A_curl·Z)dt + σdW.
- Ψ and σ are identified by #1.
- A_curl is identified in the linear setting by #2.
- Readout from programs to genes uses a masked or nonnegative U to remove the rotation symmetry (L16).
- Fit it with a multi-marginal bridge using a learned mvOU reference (#6, #7).
- Fix growth with lineage data or counts (#8).
- Validate with held-out timepoints.
- Do not claim nonlinear curl or extrapolation as identified.
